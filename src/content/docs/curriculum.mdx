The sidebar is ordered, not alphabetical. Sections are numbered in prerequisite
order: **nothing in a section depends on a section below it**, and within a
section pages are ordered the same way. Read top to bottom and you will never hit
a term that has not been introduced.

That is a real constraint, enforced by a check in the build — `npm run
check:order` fails if any page lists a prerequisite from a later section. It is
the reason a few pages sit somewhere you might not expect: **AI Safety** is in
Training rather than Foundations because it only makes sense after RLHF, and
**Embeddings** comes after Architectures because text embedding models are
transformers.

Reading everything is about **30 hours**. Most people should not do that; the
tracks below are the shorter routes.

## Three tracks

Pick the one that matches why you are here. Each is a complete, self-contained
route — the pages are still in prerequisite order within the track.

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="New to the field">

**About 8 hours.** Enough to read a modern paper's introduction and follow a
technical conversation without bluffing.

<Steps>

1. [How to read a page](/how-to-read/) — 5 min. What the four tiers mean.
2. [Backpropagation](/foundations/backpropagation/) — 30 min. How learning happens at all.
3. [Probability for ML](/foundations/probability-for-ml/) — 30 min. The notation everything else uses.
4. [Tokenization](/foundations/tokenization/) — 35 min. What a model actually sees. Explains several famous "failures" that are not reasoning failures.
5. [Representations](/foundations/representations/) — 35 min. The central idea of the field.
6. [Encoders](/encoders/) — 25 min. The word means three different things; sort them out early.
7. [Neural Encoders](/encoders/neural-encoders/) — 45 min. Autoencoders through VAEs, VQ-VAE and contrastive.
8. [Transformers](/architectures/transformers/) — 45 min. The architecture behind nearly everything current.
9. [Optimization](/training/optimization/) — 35 min. Why Adam, why warmup, why any of it works.
10. [Self-Supervised Learning](/training/self-supervision/) — 40 min. Where the training signal comes from without labels.
11. [Post-Training and Alignment](/training/post-training/) — 40 min. How a raw pretrained model becomes an assistant.
12. [Generalization](/foundations/generalization/) — 30 min. Why overparameterised models work, which classical theory said they should not.
13. [Evaluation](/foundations/evaluation/) — 30 min. How to tell whether a reported number means anything.
14. [Hardware — Start Here](/hardware/) — 12 min. What things cost, in one page.

</Steps>

Stop at any tier of any page. Tier 1 of all fourteen is roughly three hours and
still leaves you with a correct picture.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Heading for research">

**About 20 hours.** The full path to reading current papers and having opinions

Everything in the first track, then:

<Steps>

2. [State Representations](/foundations/state-representations/) — 45 min. What a state must contain, and the sufficiency argument.
3. [Encoder–Decoder Architectures](/encoders/encoder-decoder/) — 35 min.
4. [State Space Models](/architectures/state-space-models/) — 35 min. The credible attention alternative.
5. [Mixture of Experts](/architectures/mixture-of-experts/) — 35 min. Decoupling parameters from compute.

7. [Diffusion Models](/architectures/diffusion/) — 45 min.
8. [Energy-Based Models](/architectures/energy-based-models/) — 55 min. The framing that unifies most of the above.
9. [JEPA](/architectures/jepa/) — 40 min. The non-generative alternative, and the argument for it.
10. [Multimodal Models](/architectures/multimodal/) — 35 min.
11. [Fine-Tuning and PEFT](/training/fine-tuning/) — 30 min.
12. [Reinforcement Learning](/training/reinforcement-learning/) — 40 min.

14. [AI Safety and Alignment](/training/ai-safety/) — 35 min.
15. [Interpretability](/foundations/interpretability/) — 40 min. Superposition, circuits, sparse autoencoders.

<TabItem label="Building a system">

**About 9 hours.** The path for someone shipping something rather than writing a

<Steps>

2. [Tokenization](/foundations/tokenization/) — 35 min. Directly explains cost, context limits and several classes of bug.

6. [Retrieval and RAG](/systems/retrieval/) — 35 min. The pipeline and where each stage fails.
7. [Fine-Tuning and PEFT](/training/fine-tuning/) — 30 min. LoRA, QLoRA, and when not to bother.

## How the order is maintained

Ordering that is not enforced decays. Three mechanisms keep this one honest:

<Steps>

1. **`sidebar.order` in every page's frontmatter.** Index pages are always 0, and no two pages in a directory may share a number — Starlight breaks ties by filename, which is not a curriculum.

2. **`prereqs` in frontmatter**, rendered at the top of each page. These are the actual dependency edges.

3. **`npm run check:order`**, which fails the build if any page has no order, if two pages in a directory collide, if an index page is not 0, or — the important one — if any page lists a prerequisite from a *later* section. A forward prerequisite means the section order is wrong, and the check makes that impossible to ignore.

</Steps>

If you add a page, place it by asking what the reader must already know, not by
what it is about. The check will tell you if you got it wrong.
