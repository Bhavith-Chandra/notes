<Abstract>
This section answers two questions that get asked constantly and answered badly:
*what hardware do we need*, and *how much compute does this cost*. Both have
arithmetic answers. This page is the cheat sheet — the numbers you need in a
meeting. The three pages beneath it derive those numbers, explain when they
break, and work through the cases in full.
</Abstract>

<KeyPoints>

- **Training FLOPs ≈ 6 × parameters × tokens.** Divide by hardware throughput times ~40% utilisation to get seconds.
- **Memory, not compute, is what usually stops you.** Full fine-tuning with Adam costs ~16 bytes per parameter; inference in FP16 costs ~2.
- **Bandwidth, not FLOPs, is what usually limits you.** Most deep-learning kernels finish waiting on memory, not arithmetic.
- **Rent unless you have measured sustained utilisation.** Break-even against a workstation is around 8,000–10,000 GPU-hours of real use.

</KeyPoints>

## The four formulas

<Steps>

1. **Training compute.** $C \approx 6ND$ FLOPs, for $N$ parameters and $D$ tokens. Forward-only (inference prefill) is $2ND$.

2. **Wall clock.** $T = 6ND / (n \cdot X \cdot u)$, with $n$ accelerators of peak throughput $X$ at utilisation $u$. Use $u = 0.4$ for planning, then measure.

3. **Training memory.** About **16 bytes per parameter** for mixed-precision Adam — 2 for BF16 weights, 2 for gradients, 12 for FP32 master weights and the two Adam moments — plus activations.

4. **Inference memory.** Weights (2 bytes per parameter in FP16, ~0.6 in 4-bit) plus the KV cache, $2 L n_{\text{kv}} d_{\text{head}} s b \cdot \text{bytes}$, which at long context or high batch often exceeds the weights.

</Steps>

## Lookup: what you need, by model size

Approximate, and sufficient for a first decision. "Serve" assumes moderate batch
and 4k–8k context; longer context needs more for the KV cache.

| Model | Serve, 4-bit | Serve, FP16 | LoRA fine-tune | Full fine-tune |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3B | 2–4 GB · any modern GPU | 6 GB · any modern GPU | 8 GB · one consumer card | 48 GB · one A100/H100 |
| 7–8B | ~6 GB · one 8 GB card | 16 GB · one 24 GB card | 12–16 GB · one 24 GB card | ~112 GB · 2–4 GPUs, FSDP |
| 13–14B | ~10 GB · one 12 GB card | 28 GB · one 40 GB card | ~20 GB · one 24 GB card | ~208 GB · 4–8 GPUs |
| 30–34B | ~22 GB · one 24 GB card | 68 GB · one 80 GB card | ~36 GB · one 48 GB card | ~500 GB · one 8-GPU node |
| 70B | ~42 GB · one 48 GB card | 140 GB · 2× 80 GB | ~70 GB · one 80 GB card | ~1.1 TB · 16+ GPUs |
| 400B+ | ~240 GB · one 8-GPU node | 800 GB · 8–16 GPUs | ~420 GB · one 8-GPU node | multi-node cluster |

Read across a row and notice how far apart the columns are. The same 70B model is
a single-card job to serve in 4-bit and a small-cluster job to fully fine-tune.
Most disagreements about "what hardware do we need" are actually disagreements
about which column you are in.

## Lookup: what a job costs

Using $6ND$, 40% MFU on H100s, and $3.50 per GPU-hour.

| Job | FLOPs | Hardware | Time | Cost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| LoRA 7B on 50M tokens | 1.4 × 10¹⁸ | 1× H100 | ~1.3 h | under $10 |
| Full fine-tune 7B on 50M tokens | 2.1 × 10¹⁸ | 4× H100 | ~30 min | ~$15 |
| Fine-tune 70B on 1B tokens | 4.2 × 10²⁰ | 16× H100 | ~18 h | ~$1,000 |
| Pretrain 7B on 1T tokens | 4.2 × 10²² | 64× H100 | ~19 days | ~$103,000 |
| Pretrain 70B on 15T tokens | 6.3 × 10²⁴ | 1024× H100 | ~160 days | ~$12M |

Budget two to three times these figures in practice. The estimate covers the
successful run; it does not cover failed runs, restarts, hyperparameter search,
data-pipeline compute or evaluation, and those are the normal case rather than
the exception.
