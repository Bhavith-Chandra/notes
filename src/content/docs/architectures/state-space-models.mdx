<Abstract>
A state space model is a recurrent network with the recurrence chosen so it can
also be evaluated in parallel. That single property — recurrent at inference,
convolutional at training — is what let SSMs escape the reason RNNs lost. Mamba
then added input-dependent state updates, which fixed the quality gap and broke
the parallel form, requiring a hardware-aware scan to recover it. The remaining
weakness is exact recall, and it is structural rather than incidental.
</Abstract>

<KeyPoints>

- **The trick is dual evaluation.** With a *linear time-invariant* recurrence, the whole sequence can be computed as a single convolution — parallel like a transformer at training time, $O(1)$ per token at inference like an RNN.
- **Selectivity fixed the quality gap and cost the convolution.** Making $A$, $B$, $C$ input-dependent lets the model decide what to remember; it also makes the system time-varying, so the convolution no longer exists and you need a parallel scan instead.
- **A fixed-size state is the whole trade.** Attention keeps every token and can retrieve any of them exactly. An SSM compresses history into $d_{\text{state}}$ numbers. That is why SSMs win on throughput and lose on copying.
- **SSMs and attention are formally related.** Mamba-2's structured state space duality shows both are instances of one framework with different structure imposed on the same semiseparable matrix — so the choice is about which structure, not about two unrelated ideas.

</KeyPoints>

## Taking notes versus keeping the transcript

Two ways to follow a long lecture.

**Keep the full transcript.** Any time you need something, look it up exactly. This
is attention. Perfect recall, and the cost of consulting your notes grows with how
long the lecture has been.

**Keep a running summary.** Each new sentence, update your notes — add what
matters, let the rest go. This is a state space model. Your notes never get longer,
so each update costs the same whether you are ten minutes or ten hours in. But the
exact wording of a sentence from an hour ago is gone unless you happened to write
it down.

**That is the entire trade-off**, and everything else on this page follows from it.

## Why RNNs lost, and what changed

Classic RNNs also kept a running summary, and they were abandoned around 2017 for a
specific reason: **you cannot parallelise them across the sequence.** To compute
the state at position 500 you must first compute 499. On a GPU that is fatal —
transformers process all positions at once, so they train vastly faster on the
same hardware.

The SSM insight is that if you restrict the recurrence to a particular *linear*
form, the same computation can be written as a **convolution** — and convolutions
parallelise perfectly.

So you train it as a convolution (fast, parallel) and run it as a recurrence
(fast, constant memory). Same weights, two evaluation modes. That is what put
recurrence back on the table.

<Aside type="tip" title="Why linearity is not a crippling restriction">
The recurrence is linear, but the model is not. Nonlinearity sits *between* SSM
layers, exactly as it sits between attention layers in a transformer. The linear
recurrence is a component, not the whole model.
</Aside>

## The complication Mamba fixed

A pure linear time-invariant SSM has a problem for language: it treats every token
the same way. The update rule is fixed, so it cannot decide that *this* word is
important and *that* one is filler. On dense, structured signals like audio that is
fine. On text it is a real handicap, and it is why early SSMs were strong on long-
range benchmarks and weak on language modelling.

Mamba made the update **input-dependent** — the model looks at the incoming token
and decides how much to remember and how much to forget. That closed most of the
quality gap.

The cost: input-dependence breaks time-invariance, and without time-invariance
there is no convolution. Mamba's engineering contribution is a hardware-aware
**parallel scan** that recovers parallel training anyway, by keeping the state in
fast on-chip memory and never writing the intermediate states out.

## The lineage

<Steps>

1. **HiPPO (2020).** Asks a precise question: if you must compress a signal's whole
   history into a fixed-size state, what is the *optimal* way? The answer is
   projection onto a basis of orthogonal polynomials, and it yields a specific
   structured matrix $A$. This is where the initialisation comes from — it is
   derived, not tuned.

2. **S4 (2021).** Makes HiPPO practical. The naive convolution kernel requires
   powers of a large matrix; S4 uses a diagonal-plus-low-rank parameterisation to
   compute it efficiently. Dominated the Long Range Arena benchmark, and remained
   clearly behind transformers on language.

3. **Mamba (2023).** Makes $B$, $C$, and the discretisation step $\Delta$ functions
   of the input. Language quality becomes competitive with transformers at
   comparable scale. Requires the hardware-aware scan to stay trainable.

4. **Mamba-2 (2024).** Proves the structured state space duality — that attention
   and SSMs are two structural specialisations of the same object — and uses it to
   get a much faster implementation built on matrix multiplication.

</Steps>

## Selectivity, concretely

Consider a copying task: read a sequence, then reproduce a specific earlier token
on demand.

A time-invariant SSM applies the same update to every token. It has no mechanism to
say "hold this one". It must compress everything uniformly, and the target token
decays along with the filler.

A selective SSM computes $\Delta_t$ from the input. Large $\Delta_t$ means "this
matters, update the state strongly"; small $\Delta_t$ means "ignore, keep the state
as is". The model gains an input-conditional gate on its own memory.

<Aside type="caution" title="Selectivity narrows the gap; it does not close it">
Even with perfect gating, the state is $d_{\text{state}}$ numbers. If a task
requires reproducing 5,000 tokens verbatim, and the state holds the equivalent of
far fewer, the information is not there. Attention has it, because attention keeps
everything. **This is an information-capacity argument, not an optimisation
one** — which is why it does not go away with better training.
</Aside>

## The comparison

| | Attention | SSM (Mamba) |
|---|---|---|
| Training time | $O(L^2 d)$ | $O(L d^2)$ |
| Inference per token | $O(L d)$ — grows with context | $O(d^2)$ — constant |
| Inference memory | KV cache, grows with $L$ | fixed state |
| Exact recall from context | yes | limited by state size |
| In-context learning | strong | weaker |
| Very long sequences | expensive | natural fit |
| Ecosystem maturity | enormous | growing |

The inference row is the commercial argument. A transformer's KV cache at 100k
tokens can exceed the model weights; an SSM's state does not grow at all.

## The continuous system

A linear state space model in continuous time:

$$
h'(t) = A\,h(t) + B\,x(t), \qquad y(t) = C\,h(t) + D\,x(t),
$$

with $h(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N}$ the hidden state. This is classical control theory —
the machinery is a century old, and the contribution of this literature is what to
do with it.

## Discretisation

To use it on a token sequence, discretise with step size $\Delta$. The
zero-order-hold discretisation gives

$$
\bar A = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad \bar B = (\Delta A)^{-1}\big(\exp(\Delta A) - I\big)\,\Delta B,
$$

$$
h_t = \bar A\, h_{t-1} + \bar B\, x_t, \qquad y_t = C\,h_t .
$$

$\Delta$ has an interpretation worth carrying: it is the **resolution** at which
the model samples the underlying continuous signal. Large $\Delta$ means each
token is a big jump, so the state turns over quickly and old information decays
fast; small $\Delta$ means fine sampling and long memory. Making $\Delta$
input-dependent is therefore making *memory length* input-dependent.

## The convolutional form

Unroll the recurrence from $h_{-1} = 0$:

$$
h_t = \sum_{k=0}^{t} \bar A^{\,k}\,\bar B\, x_{t-k} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y_t = \sum_{k=0}^{t} C\bar A^{\,k}\bar B\, x_{t-k}.
$$

That is a convolution $y = \bar K * x$ with kernel

$$
\bar K = \big(C\bar B,\ C\bar A\bar B,\ C\bar A^2\bar B,\ \dots,\ C\bar A^{L-1}\bar B\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{L}.
$$

Once $\bar K$ is known, the convolution costs $O(L\log L)$ by FFT and is fully
parallel. **The equivalence requires $\bar A, \bar B, C$ to be independent of $t$**
— the moment they depend on the input, $\bar K$ is different at every position and
the convolution does not exist.

## S4's structured parameterisation

Computing $\bar K$ naively needs $L$ powers of an $N\times N$ matrix: $O(N^2 L)$
time and $O(NL)$ memory, which is prohibitive.

S4 restricts $A$ to a **diagonal plus low-rank (DPLR)** form,

$$
A = \Lambda - P Q^{*},
$$

for diagonal $\Lambda$ and low-rank $PQ^*$. Under this structure the kernel's
generating function can be evaluated via a Cauchy kernel, reducing the cost to
$\tilde O(N + L)$. Later work (S4D) showed the purely diagonal case captures most
of the benefit, which considerably simplified implementations.

The **HiPPO matrix** supplies the initialisation. HiPPO derives the $A$ that makes
the state an optimal projection of the input history onto Legendre polynomials
under a chosen measure. Initialising randomly instead measurably destroys
long-range performance — this is one of the rare cases where an initialisation is
derived from first principles and demonstrably matters.

## Mamba's selection mechanism

Make the parameters functions of the input:

$$
B_t = s_B(x_t), \qquad C_t = s_C(x_t), \qquad \Delta_t = \operatorname{softplus}\big(s_\Delta(x_t)\big),
$$

with $s_B, s_C, s_\Delta$ linear projections. The recurrence becomes time-varying:

$$
h_t = \bar A_t\, h_{t-1} + \bar B_t\, x_t .
$$

Note $A$ itself stays input-independent; the time-variation enters through
$\Delta_t$ in $\bar A_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)$. The gating interpretation is direct:

$$
\Delta_t \to \infty \implies \bar A_t \to 0,\ \text{state resets, input dominates},
$$
$$
\Delta_t \to 0 \implies \bar A_t \to I,\ \text{state persists, input ignored}.
$$

**The selective scan.** Since the convolution is gone, Mamba computes the
recurrence with a parallel associative scan — the same primitive as a parallel
prefix sum, $O(\log L)$ depth. The engineering contribution is keeping the expanded
state ($B \times L \times D \times N$) in SRAM and recomputing it in the backward
pass rather than materialising it in HBM, which is the same IO-aware idea that
makes FlashAttention fast.

## Structured state space duality

Mamba-2's theoretical result. Write the SSM's input-output map as a matrix $M$
with $y = Mx$. From the unrolled recurrence,

$$
M_{ij} = C_i^{\top}\left(\prod_{k=j+1}^{i} \bar A_k\right)\bar B_j \quad \text{for } i \geq j,\qquad M_{ij}=0 \text{ otherwise}.
$$

This is a **semiseparable** matrix: every submatrix drawn from the lower triangle
has rank at most $N$. Meanwhile masked attention is

$$
M^{\text{attn}} = \big(L \circ QK^{\top}\big),
$$

with $L$ the causal mask. Both are structured matrices with a rank constraint on
their off-diagonal blocks; the SSM imposes a sequential-product structure and
attention imposes a low-rank one.

Practically, this let Mamba-2 restructure the scan into block-matrix
multiplications that hit tensor cores, giving a large speedup over Mamba-1 — the
theory paid off as an implementation.

## The capacity argument, made precise

Consider associative recall: store $k$ key-value pairs, then answer a query.

- **Attention** stores all $k$ pairs in the KV cache. Retrieval is exact for any
  $k$, at the cost of $O(k)$ memory.
- **An SSM** has $N$ state dimensions. It can store at most $O(N)$ pairs before
  interference. For $k \gg N$, retrieval degrades — not because of a training
  failure but because the information was never retained.

This is why SSMs underperform on tasks requiring verbatim copying from long
context, and it is a capacity bound rather than a tuning problem. It also predicts
the fix: **interleave a few attention layers**, which is exactly what hybrids do.

## Where this stands, as of 2026

**Hybrids are winning, and that is the honest headline.** Jamba and similar
architectures interleave a small number of attention layers among many
Mamba layers — typically around one in eight. The attention layers supply exact
recall; the SSM layers supply cheap bulk sequence processing. These
consistently beat both pure architectures, which tells you the two mechanisms are
complementary rather than competing.

**Pure SSMs match transformers on perplexity and lag on recall-heavy tasks.** The
language-modelling loss gap has essentially closed at matched scale. The gap that
remains is task-specific and shows up on long-context retrieval, in-context
learning with many examples, and anything requiring verbatim copying.

**The inference economics are genuinely compelling.** Constant memory per token and
no KV cache is a large operational advantage at long context, and it is the reason
serving-focused teams keep investing here regardless of benchmark parity.

**Nobody claims attention is obsolete any more.** The 2023-era framing of Mamba as
a replacement has settled into a more accurate picture: attention is one structure
on a semiseparable matrix, SSMs are another, and real systems use both.

## Open problems

- **The recall gap is structural.** Fixed state cannot hold unbounded context. Any
  fix either grows the state (losing the efficiency) or adds attention (which is
  what hybrids do). No third option has appeared.

- **The optimal hybrid ratio is empirical.** One attention layer in eight is a
  measured default, not a derived one. It presumably depends on task and scale, and
  nothing predicts it.

- **In-context learning is weaker and the reason is unclear.** Induction heads —
  the attention circuits associated with in-context learning — have no clean SSM
  analogue. Whether SSMs implement something equivalent differently, or genuinely
  lack the capability, is open.

- **The ecosystem gap is real and underrated.** Quantisation, LoRA, speculative
  decoding, and most serving infrastructure were built for transformers. Each needs
  separate work for SSMs, and that adoption cost is a bigger practical barrier than
  the quality gap.

- **Scaling laws are not mapped.** No SSM equivalent of Chinchilla exists, so
  compute-optimal training for these architectures is guesswork.
