<Abstract>
Every generative model answers the same question — *what does the data
distribution look like, and can I draw from it?* — and every one of them makes a
trade among three things you would like at once: exact likelihoods, high sample
quality, and fast sampling. **No family gets all three.** That constraint, not
architectural fashion, explains why the field cycled through GANs, flows and
diffusion, and why autoregressive models never went away.
</Abstract>

<KeyPoints>

- **The generative trilemma organises everything.** Fast sampling, high quality, tractable likelihood — pick two. GANs sacrifice likelihood, flows sacrifice quality, diffusion sacrificed speed until distillation, autoregressive models sacrifice speed permanently.
- **The likelihood/quality gap is not a bug in either metric.** A model can have excellent likelihood and produce terrible samples, and vice versa. Likelihood is dominated by how well you cover the *tails*; perceptual quality is dominated by the *mode* you land in.
- **GANs are not dead, they are specialised.** One-step sampling remains unmatched, and for real-time or on-device generation that still decides it. What killed them for general use was training instability and mode collapse, not sample quality.
- **Diffusion won by decomposing the hard problem.** A GAN learns one violent jump from noise to image against an adversary. Diffusion learns a thousand tiny denoising steps, each an ordinary regression — stable, scalable, and slow. Every recent advance attacks the slowness.

</KeyPoints>

## What all of them are doing

You have a pile of photographs. Somewhere out there is a probability distribution
$p_{\text{data}}$ that made them. You want two things from a model of it:

- **Sample.** Produce new photographs that could plausibly have come from the same
  pile.
- **Score.** Say how likely a given photograph is under the distribution.

Some families do both. Some do only the first, and cannot tell you how likely
anything is. Which one you need decides most of your choice.

## Why likelihood and beauty come apart

This confuses people, and the explanation is worth having.

Likelihood is an average of $\log p(x)$ over held-out data. It is dominated by the
*worst* cases — a model that assigns near-zero probability to a few real images
takes an enormous penalty, because $\log 0 = -\infty$. So a good likelihood forces
you to **cover everything**, including the strange tails.

Perceptual quality is judged on what you *produce*. A model that generates only
the most typical, safest images looks excellent and has ignored most of the
distribution.

**So the two metrics reward opposite behaviours.** A model that covers everything
badly beats a model that covers a little superbly on likelihood, and loses on
FID. Neither metric is wrong; they measure different things, and reporting only
one is how a great deal of generative-modelling progress got overstated.

<Aside type="tip" title="The one-line diagnosis">
Blurry outputs mean the model is *mode-covering* — spreading probability over
everything, which is what maximum likelihood asks for. Sharp but repetitive
outputs mean it is *mode-seeking* — committing to a few modes, which is what an
adversarial loss encourages. This is the same forward/reverse KL asymmetry from
[probability](/foundations/probability-for-ml/), showing up as a visible artefact.
</Aside>

## The six families

<Tabs>
<TabItem label="GAN">

**Two networks in a game.** A generator maps noise to images; a discriminator tries
to tell real from fake. The generator's loss is the discriminator's failure.

**What it gives you.** One-step sampling — a single forward pass — and, at its
peak, the best-looking images anyone had produced. StyleGAN2 faces were the state
of the art for years.

**What it costs.** No likelihood. Ever. The model never represents $p(x)$; it only
produces samples, which makes it an *implicit* model. You cannot score, cannot
detect out-of-distribution inputs, cannot compress.

**The failure modes, both structural:**

- **Mode collapse.** The generator finds one output that fools the discriminator
  and produces only that. It is winning the game as defined, and the game was
  defined wrong.
- **Non-convergence.** This is a minimax problem, not a minimisation. There is no
  loss that decreases monotonically, so you cannot tell from the curves whether
  training is going well — you have to look at samples.

**The fixes that mattered.** WGAN replaced the Jensen–Shannon objective with an
Earth-Mover distance that gives useful gradients even when the distributions do not
overlap. WGAN-GP replaced weight clipping with a gradient penalty. Spectral
normalisation constrains the discriminator's Lipschitz constant cheaply and became
standard.

**Use it today for**: real-time and on-device generation, super-resolution,
image-to-image translation (pix2pix, CycleGAN), and as the perceptual loss inside
[neural audio codecs](/domains/speech-audio/) and VQ-GAN-style tokenisers — which
is where most GAN training now actually happens.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="VAE">

**Encode to a distribution, decode from a sample, regularise toward a prior.**
Covered fully on [Neural Encoders](/encoders/neural-encoders/).

**What it gives you.** A smooth, samplable latent space; fast one-pass sampling; a
*bound* on likelihood; and a genuinely useful encoder — the only family here where
inference is built in.

**What it costs.** Blur. The reconstruction term is typically Gaussian, so the
decoder outputs a conditional mean, and the mean of several plausible images is not
an image. Plus posterior collapse when the decoder is strong enough to ignore the
latent.

**Its real legacy is as a component.** VQ-VAE tokenisers feed
[diffusion](/architectures/diffusion/) and [audio models](/domains/speech-audio/);
latent diffusion runs inside a VAE's latent space. The VAE mostly stopped being an
endpoint and became infrastructure.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Normalizing flow">

**Build the distribution from an invertible chain.** Start with a simple
distribution, apply a sequence of invertible transformations, and use the
change-of-variables formula to get an *exact* density.

**What it gives you.** Exact likelihood — not a bound, the actual number — plus
fast sampling and exact inference. Uniquely among these families, all three of
those at once.

**What it costs.** The transformations must be invertible with a tractable Jacobian
determinant, and that constraint is severe. Every layer must preserve
dimensionality — no bottleneck, ever — and the architectures you are allowed
(coupling layers, autoregressive flows) are far less expressive per parameter than
an unconstrained network.

**The result**: flows have never matched GAN or diffusion sample quality on images
at comparable compute.

**Use it for**: density estimation proper, out-of-distribution detection,
variational inference (as flexible posteriors), simulation-based inference in the
sciences, and lossless compression. Anywhere the *number* matters more than the
picture.

**Continuous flows** (Neural ODE, FFJORD) replace the discrete chain with an ODE,
trading the architectural constraint for numerical integration cost — and they are
the direct ancestor of [flow matching](/architectures/diffusion/).

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Autoregressive">

**Factorise the joint into a product of conditionals** and model each one. For
images, predict pixel $i$ given pixels $1..i-1$; for text, the same over tokens.

**What it gives you.** Exact likelihood, stable maximum-likelihood training with no
adversary, and — as language models demonstrated — the cleanest scaling behaviour
of any family here.

**What it costs.** Sampling is inherently sequential: $n$ forward passes for $n$
elements. For a 1024×1024 image that is a million sequential steps, which is why
autoregressive image models lost to diffusion despite better likelihoods.

**Where the ordering bites.** Text has a natural order. Images do not — raster
order forces the model to predict a pixel from the ones above and to the left,
which is an arbitrary and unnatural conditioning structure. PixelCNN's masked
convolutions have a blind spot for exactly this reason.

**Use it for**: anything with a natural sequential order. Which is to say: all of
language modelling, and — via [neural codecs](/domains/speech-audio/) — audio.
It never went away; it moved domains.

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Diffusion">

**Destroy data with noise, learn to reverse it.** Covered in full on
[Diffusion Models](/architectures/diffusion/).

**Why it won.** It decomposed an impossible problem into a thousand easy ones. Each
denoising step is an ordinary supervised regression with a squared loss — no
adversary, no equilibrium, no invertibility constraint. Training is about as stable
as training a classifier, and it scales.

**What it costs.** Sampling. Hundreds of network evaluations per image at first;
DDIM, distillation, consistency models and rectified flow have pushed that toward
one, each by adding machinery.

**Use it for**: essentially all high-quality image, video and audio generation
today, plus as a policy class in robotics and as a
[world model](/world-models/catalogue/).

</TabItem>
<TabItem label="Energy-based">

**Define an energy $E_\theta(x)$ and let $p(x) \propto e^{-E(x)}$.** Maximum
flexibility — any scalar function of $x$ defines a valid distribution.

**What it costs, and it is fatal for most uses.** The normalising constant $Z =
\int e^{-E(x)}dx$ is intractable. You cannot compute the likelihood, and training
by maximum likelihood requires sampling from the model itself at every step,
usually via MCMC, which is slow and unreliable in high dimensions.

**Why it still matters.** The score function $\nabla_x \log p(x) = -\nabla_x E(x)$
does not involve $Z$ at all — the gradient of the log-density is computable even
when the density is not. **That single observation is the bridge to score matching
and therefore to diffusion.** Modern diffusion models are, in a precise sense, the
energy-based programme made trainable.

</TabItem>
</Tabs>

## The comparison

| | Likelihood | Sampling | Quality | Training stability | Inference (encoder) | Alive in 2026 for |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **GAN** | none | **1 step** | high | **poor** (minimax) | no | real-time, on-device, codecs |
| **VAE** | bound | 1 step | low (blurry) | good | **yes** | tokenisers, latent spaces |
| **Flow** | **exact** | 1 step | moderate | good | **yes** (exact) | density estimation, OOD, science |
| **Autoregressive** | **exact** | $n$ steps | high | **best** | no | language, audio, anything ordered |
| **Diffusion** | bound | 10–1000 steps | **highest** | good | no | images, video, audio, policies |
| **Energy-based** | unnormalised | MCMC | moderate | poor | no | conceptual bridge to diffusion |

<Aside type="danger" title="FID is the field's most over-trusted number">
FID compares Inception-v3 feature statistics between real and generated sets. It is
biased by sample count (fewer samples inflates it), sensitive to image
preprocessing and JPEG artefacts, and inherits whatever ImageNet classification
happened to make salient. It also collapses two distinct failures — poor fidelity
and poor diversity — into one scalar, so a mode-collapsed model and a blurry one
can score alike. Report **precision and recall separately** if you want to know
which is happening.
</Aside>

## GAN: the minimax game and what it optimises

$$
\min_G \max_D\ V(D,G) = \mathbb{E}_{x\sim p_{\text{data}}}\big[\log D(x)\big] + \mathbb{E}_{z\sim p_z}\big[\log\big(1 - D(G(z))\big)\big].
$$

**The optimal discriminator, for fixed $G$.** Differentiating the integrand
pointwise:

$$
D^{*}(x) = \frac{p_{\text{data}}(x)}{p_{\text{data}}(x) + p_g(x)} .
$$

Substituting back gives

$$
V(D^{*}, G) = 2\,\mathrm{JSD}\big(p_{\text{data}} \,\|\, p_g\big) - \log 4 ,
$$

so the generator is minimising the **Jensen–Shannon divergence**. Global optimum at
$p_g = p_{\text{data}}$, value $-\log 4$.

**Why this fails in practice.** If $p_{\text{data}}$ and $p_g$ have disjoint
support — which is generic when both are supported on low-dimensional manifolds in
pixel space — then $\mathrm{JSD} = \log 2$ **constantly**, and its gradient with
respect to $G$ is zero. A perfect discriminator provides no learning signal at all.
This is not an optimisation difficulty; the objective is genuinely flat.

**WGAN's fix.** Replace JSD with the Wasserstein-1 distance, which under the
Kantorovich–Rubinstein duality is

$$
W_1(p_{\text{data}}, p_g) = \sup_{\|f\|_{L}\leq 1}\ \mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}[f(x)] - \mathbb{E}_{p_g}[f(x)] .
$$

$W_1$ is finite and has non-vanishing gradient even for disjoint supports —
it measures *how far apart* the distributions are, not merely that they differ.
The critic $f$ must be 1-Lipschitz, enforced by weight clipping (WGAN), a gradient
penalty (WGAN-GP),

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{GP}} = \lambda\,\mathbb{E}_{\hat x}\Big[\big(\|\nabla_{\hat x} f(\hat x)\|_2 - 1\big)^2\Big],
$$

or by constraining each layer's spectral norm (spectral normalisation), which is
the cheapest and became standard.

**A useful side effect**: $W_1$ correlates with sample quality, so the critic loss
is an actual progress signal — something the original GAN objective never provided.

## Normalizing flows: change of variables

For an invertible $f_\theta$ with $z = f_\theta(x)$:

$$
\log p_X(x) = \log p_Z\big(f_\theta(x)\big) + \log\left|\det \frac{\partial f_\theta}{\partial x}\right| .
$$

The whole difficulty is the log-determinant: computing it naively is $O(d^3)$,
which is impossible at image dimensionality.

**Coupling layers solve it by construction.** Split $x$ into two halves and
transform only one, conditioned on the other:

$$
y_{1:k} = x_{1:k}, \qquad y_{k+1:d} = x_{k+1:d}\odot \exp\big(s(x_{1:k})\big) + t(x_{1:k}).
$$

The Jacobian is triangular:

$$
\frac{\partial y}{\partial x} = \begin{bmatrix} I & 0 \\ \frac{\partial y_{k+1:d}}{\partial x_{1:k}} & \operatorname{diag}\big(\exp(s)\big)\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
\log\left|\det\frac{\partial y}{\partial x}\right| = \sum_{j} s_j(x_{1:k}).
$$

**The determinant is a sum** — $O(d)$, not $O(d^3)$ — and $s, t$ can be arbitrarily
complex networks because they are never inverted. Inversion is trivial:

$$
x_{k+1:d} = \big(y_{k+1:d} - t(y_{1:k})\big)\odot \exp\big(-s(y_{1:k})\big).
$$

**Where the expressiveness goes.** Each layer only transforms half the dimensions,
so you need many layers and alternating masks. And the dimension can never change:
no bottleneck, no compression, every layer is $d\times d$. That is the structural
reason flows underperform on images — they are spending capacity maintaining
invertibility that the task never asked for.

**Glow's contribution** was replacing the fixed permutation between coupling layers
with a learned invertible $1\times1$ convolution, whose determinant is $h\cdot w
\cdot \log|\det W|$ for a $c\times c$ matrix $W$ — cheap, and it lets the model
learn which channels to mix.

## Autoregressive: exact factorisation

$$
p(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p\big(x_i \mid x_{<i}\big), \qquad \mathcal{L} = -\sum_{i} \log p_\theta\big(x_i\mid x_{<i}\big).
$$

Exact by the chain rule of probability — no approximation anywhere, which is why
the likelihood is exact and training is stable.

**The cost is structural.** Sampling requires $n$ sequential evaluations, and no
amount of hardware parallelism removes a sequential dependency. Training
*is* parallel (teacher forcing evaluates all conditionals at once), which produces
the characteristic asymmetry: fast to train, slow to sample — the exact reverse of
diffusion.

**Discretised logistic mixture.** PixelCNN++'s practically important detail:
modelling a 256-way softmax per channel wastes capacity, because pixel values 127
and 128 are nearly the same and a softmax treats them as unrelated categories.
Instead model a continuous mixture and integrate over each discrete bin:

$$
p(x = v) = \sum_{k} \pi_k\Big[\sigma\big(\tfrac{v + 0.5 - \mu_k}{s_k}\big) - \sigma\big(\tfrac{v - 0.5 - \mu_k}{s_k}\big)\Big],
$$

which encodes ordinality, uses far fewer parameters, and improves likelihood
measurably.

## Energy-based models and the bridge to diffusion

$$
p_\theta(x) = \frac{e^{-E_\theta(x)}}{Z_\theta}, \qquad Z_\theta = \int e^{-E_\theta(x)}\,dx .
$$

The maximum-likelihood gradient is

$$
\nabla_\theta \big(-\log p_\theta(x)\big) = \nabla_\theta E_\theta(x) - \mathbb{E}_{x'\sim p_\theta}\big[\nabla_\theta E_\theta(x')\big].
$$

Read the two terms: push energy **down** on real data, push it **up** on samples
from the current model. Intuitive, and it requires sampling from $p_\theta$ at
every gradient step — typically Langevin MCMC:

$$
x_{t+1} = x_t - \tfrac{\eta}{2}\nabla_x E_\theta(x_t) + \sqrt{\eta}\,\epsilon_t, \qquad \epsilon_t\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I),
$$

which mixes slowly in high dimensions and makes training fragile.

**The escape.** Note that

$$
\nabla_x \log p_\theta(x) = -\nabla_x E_\theta(x)
$$

— the $Z_\theta$ vanishes, because it does not depend on $x$. So the **score** is
computable even though the density is not. Score matching learns $s_\theta(x)
\approx \nabla_x\log p_{\text{data}}(x)$ directly, and denoising score matching
makes that tractable by adding noise:

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\text{DSM}} = \mathbb{E}_{x,\tilde x}\Big[\big\|s_\theta(\tilde x) - \nabla_{\tilde x}\log q_\sigma(\tilde x\mid x)\big\|^2\Big] = \mathbb{E}\left[\Big\|s_\theta(\tilde x) + \frac{\tilde x - x}{\sigma^2}\Big\|^2\right].
$$

**That is the diffusion objective.** The energy-based programme — flexible,
principled, untrainable — became trainable the moment the field stopped trying to
model $p$ and started modelling $\nabla_x \log p$.

## Why maximum likelihood produces blur

The MLE objective is equivalent to minimising forward KL:

$$
\arg\max_\theta \mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}\big[\log p_\theta(x)\big] = \arg\min_\theta\ \mathrm{KL}\big(p_{\text{data}}\,\|\,p_\theta\big).
$$

Forward KL is $\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}[\log p_{\text{data}}/p_\theta]$, which
is **infinite** wherever $p_{\text{data}} > 0$ and $p_\theta = 0$. The penalty for
missing a mode is unbounded; the penalty for putting mass where there is no data is
merely finite.

So MLE is **mode-covering**: it spreads probability to cover everything, including
the space between modes. For a unimodal decoder that means outputting an average,
and an average of images is a blur.

An adversarial loss approximates something closer to reverse KL, which is
**mode-seeking** — it is punished for generating implausible samples and not for
missing modes. Hence sharp, and hence mode collapse.

**These are two failure modes of one asymmetry**, and no choice of architecture
escapes it — only a choice of divergence does.

## Where this stands, as of 2026

**Diffusion and flow matching own high-quality generation**, and the two have
largely merged notationally — diffusion with a particular schedule *is* flow
matching along a curved path. New work is written in flow-matching terms because
the formulation is simpler and the straight-path property gives a principled route
to few-step sampling.

**Autoregressive owns everything with a natural order.** Language, and via
tokenisation, audio and increasingly video. The scaling behaviour is cleaner than
any other family's, and that has proven decisive at frontier scale.

**GANs survive in a well-defined niche.** One-step sampling is still unmatched
where latency is hard-constrained. And GAN losses live on *inside* other systems —
the discriminator in a neural audio codec or a VQ-GAN tokeniser is doing perceptual
work that no reconstruction loss does.

**Flows are healthy in the sciences and marginal in vision.** Simulation-based
inference, lattice field theory sampling, and molecular conformer generation all use
flows precisely because the exact likelihood is the deliverable. Nobody is trying
to beat diffusion on faces with them any more, and that is a sensible division.

**The likelihood/quality gap is now understood rather than argued about.** Papers
report FID *and* precision/recall *and* likelihood where applicable, because the
field accepted that one number cannot capture both fidelity and coverage.

## Open problems

- **Evaluation.** FID's flaws are documented and it remains the default because the
  alternatives are worse or more expensive. Human evaluation is the ground truth and
  does not scale.

- **One-step generation without a second stage.** Distillation and consistency
  models work and require training a teacher first. Nobody has a single-stage
  method that samples in one step at diffusion quality.

- **Discrete diffusion is unresolved.** The Gaussian machinery does not transfer to
  tokens, several corruption processes have been proposed, and none clearly wins —
  which matters because it is the main route to non-autoregressive text generation.

- **Memorisation.** Diffusion models provably reproduce training examples under
  some conditions, particularly for duplicated data. The relationship between
  duplication, capacity and extraction risk is only partly mapped, and it has direct
  legal consequences.

- **Controllability.** Classifier-free guidance is essential, unprincipled, and
  samples from a distribution nobody characterised. See
  [Diffusion](/architectures/diffusion/).

## Choosing one

<Card title="A decision procedure" icon="right-arrow">
**Need an exact likelihood?** Flow (continuous data) or autoregressive (discrete).
Nothing else gives you the number.

**Need one-step sampling under a hard latency budget?** GAN, or a distilled
diffusion model if you can afford the extra training stage.

**Need the best possible images, video or audio?** Diffusion or flow matching. Not
close.

**Generating something with a natural order?** Autoregressive. This is why language
models are what they are.

**Need an encoder as well as a generator?** VAE or flow — the only families with
built-in inference.

**Doing science where the density is the deliverable?** Flow. The invertibility you
pay for is exactly what you are buying.
</Card>
