Reading Order

The sidebar is ordered, not alphabetical. Sections are numbered in prerequisite order: nothing in a section depends on a section below it, and within a section pages are ordered the same way. Read top to bottom and you will never hit a term that has not been introduced.

That is a real constraint, enforced by a check in the build — npm run check:order fails if any page lists a prerequisite from a later section. It is the reason a few pages sit somewhere you might not expect: AI Safety is in Training rather than Foundations because it only makes sense after RLHF, and Embeddings comes after Architectures because text embedding models are transformers.

Reading everything is about 30 hours. Most people should not do that; the tracks below are the shorter routes.

Three tracks

Section titled “Three tracks”

Pick the one that matches why you are here. Each is a complete, self-contained route — the pages are still in prerequisite order within the track.

  • New to the field
  • Heading for research

About 8 hours. Enough to read a modern paper’s introduction and follow a technical conversation without bluffing.

  1. How to read a page — 5 min. What the four tiers mean.
  2. Backpropagation — 30 min. How learning happens at all.
  3. Probability for ML — 30 min. The notation everything else uses.
  4. Tokenization — 35 min. What a model actually sees. Explains several famous “failures” that are not reasoning failures.
  5. Representations — 35 min. The central idea of the field.
  6. Encoders — 25 min. The word means three different things; sort them out early.
  7. Neural Encoders — 45 min. Autoencoders through VAEs, VQ-VAE and contrastive.
  8. Transformers — 45 min. The architecture behind nearly everything current.
  9. Optimization — 35 min. Why Adam, why warmup, why any of it works.
  10. Self-Supervised Learning — 40 min. Where the training signal comes from without labels.
  11. Post-Training and Alignment — 40 min. How a raw pretrained model becomes an assistant.
  12. Generalization — 30 min. Why overparameterised models work, which classical theory said they should not.
  13. Evaluation — 30 min. How to tell whether a reported number means anything.
  14. Hardware — Start Here — 12 min. What things cost, in one page.

Stop at any tier of any page. Tier 1 of all fourteen is roughly three hours and still leaves you with a correct picture.

About 9 hours. The path for someone shipping something rather than writing a paper. Skips most of the theory and none of the things that break in production.

  1. Transformers — 45 min. Read at least tiers 1–2.
  2. Tokenization — 35 min. Directly explains cost, context limits and several classes of bug.
  3. Embeddings — Start Here — 28 min. The four questions that determine whether an embedding is right for you.

How the order is maintained

Section titled “How the order is maintained”

Ordering that is not enforced decays. Three mechanisms keep this one honest:

  1. sidebar.order in every page’s frontmatter. Index pages are always 0, and no two pages in a directory may share a number — Starlight breaks ties by filename, which is not a curriculum.

  2. prereqs in frontmatter, rendered at the top of each page. These are the actual dependency edges.

  3. npm run check:order, which fails the build if any page has no order, if two pages in a directory collide, if an index page is not 0, or — the important one — if any page lists a prerequisite from a later section. A forward prerequisite means the section order is wrong, and the check makes that impossible to ignore.

If you add a page, place it by asking what the reader must already know, not by what it is about. The check will tell you if you got it wrong.
