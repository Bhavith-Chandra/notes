Graph Neural Networks

A graph neural network passes messages along edges: each node repeatedly gathers information from its neighbours and updates itself. That single mechanism covers almost every GNN ever published — and it comes with a proven ceiling. Standard message passing cannot distinguish any two graphs that the Weisfeiler-Lehman colour refinement test cannot distinguish, which rules out counting triangles and telling some molecules apart.

What you should walk away with

  • Message passing is the whole framework. Aggregate from neighbours, combine with your own state, repeat. GCN, GraphSAGE, GAT and GIN differ only in the aggregate and combine functions.
  • The aggregator must be permutation-invariant, and that constrains expressivity. Neighbours have no canonical order, so you need a set function. Mean and max lose information that sum retains — which is exactly why GIN uses sum.
  • Expressivity is capped by the 1-WL test. No amount of width, depth or training lets standard message passing separate graphs 1-WL cannot. Cycle counting and many molecular distinctions fall outside it.
  • Depth hurts, for two different reasons. Oversmoothing makes all node representations converge as layers stack; oversquashing crushes exponentially many distant nodes through a fixed-width channel. Most useful GNNs are 2–4 layers.
Tier 1 · Intuition

Plain language. No equations, no prerequisites.

Why images and text don’t cover this

Section titled “Why images and text don’t cover this”

A sentence is a sequence — every token has a position, and “third from the left” means something. An image is a grid — every pixel has coordinates.

A social network has neither. A user has friends, and there is no first friend, no canonical order, no fixed count. A molecule is atoms bonded to atoms; rotate your drawing and it is the same molecule.

Graphs have structure without coordinates, and that is what needs a different architecture. Anything you compute must give the same answer if the nodes are relabelled.

Message passing

Section titled “Message passing”

The algorithm is simple enough to state completely.

Every node starts with a feature vector. Then, repeatedly:

  1. Each node collects the vectors of its neighbours.
  2. It combines them into one summary — but the summary must not depend on the order they arrived in, so it must be something like a sum, mean or max.
  3. It updates itself from its old state plus that summary.

After one round, each node knows about its immediate neighbours. After two, about neighbours-of-neighbours. After kkk rounds, each node’s representation depends on everything within kkk hops.

Depth means radius, not capacity

In a CNN, more layers mean more abstraction. In a GNN, more layers mean a larger neighbourhood — you are literally choosing how far information travels. That reframing explains why GNNs are shallow: three layers on a small-world graph already reaches most of it.

The ceiling

Section titled “The ceiling”

Here is the surprising part, and it is a theorem rather than an observation.

Standard message passing cannot distinguish certain pairs of structurally different graphs. Consider two graphs: one is a single six-node ring; the other is two separate three-node triangles. Every node in both has exactly two neighbours. Every neighbourhood looks identical at every round.

The messages are the same. The representations are the same. No GNN of this type can tell them apart, however wide, however deep, however trained.

This exactly matches the limit of a 1960s graph-isomorphism heuristic — the Weisfeiler-Lehman colour refinement test — and that correspondence is the field’s central theoretical result. It has practical consequences: counting triangles, detecting rings in molecules, and several other chemically meaningful properties sit outside what message passing can compute.

Why deeper isn’t better

Section titled “Why deeper isn’t better”

Two distinct problems, often conflated.

Oversmoothing. Each round mixes a node with its neighbours. Do this enough times and everything converges to the same value — like repeatedly averaging a picture with its blurred self until it is uniformly grey. Node representations become indistinguishable and classification collapses.

Oversquashing. To see a node kkk hops away, information must funnel through intermediate nodes. The number of nodes at distance kkk grows exponentially; the vector carrying them is fixed-width. Exponentially much information is compressed into constant space, and most of it is destroyed.

These pull in the same direction — keep it shallow — but they are different mechanisms, and the fixes differ.

Symmetric-normalised mean over the neighbourhood including self-loops:

H(l+1)=σ ⁣(D~−1/2A~ D~−1/2H(l)W(l)).H^{(l+1)} = \sigma\!\left(\tilde D^{-1/2}\tilde A\, \tilde D^{-1/2} H^{(l)} W^{(l)}\right).H(l+1)=σ(D~−1/2A~D~−1/2H(l)W(l)).

The D~−1/2\tilde D^{-1/2}D~−1/2 on both sides is the detail worth understanding. Plain D~−1A~\tilde D^{-1}\tilde AD~−1A~ (row normalisation) is a random walk; the symmetric version keeps the operator’s eigenvalues in [−1,1][-1,1][−1,1], which keeps repeated application stable. Without it, activations explode or vanish with depth.

Simple, fast, and the mean aggregation is exactly what limits its expressivity.

Two contributions that mattered more than the architecture:

Neighbour sampling. Sample a fixed number of neighbours rather than using all of them. This bounds the computation per node, which is what makes GNNs work on graphs with high-degree hubs — without it, one celebrity account with ten million followers dominates the batch.

Inductive learning. Learn an aggregation function rather than per-node embeddings, so the model generalises to nodes never seen during training. GCN as originally formulated is transductive — the whole graph must be present at training time — which is unusable for a graph that grows.

Learn attention weights over neighbours instead of fixing them:

αij=exp⁡(LeakyReLU(a⊤[Whi ∥ Whj]))∑k∈N(i)exp⁡(LeakyReLU(a⊤[Whi ∥ Whk])).\alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp\big(\text{LeakyReLU}(a^{\top}[Wh_i \,\|\, Wh_j])\big)}{\sum_{k\in\mathcal{N}(i)}\exp\big(\text{LeakyReLU}(a^{\top}[Wh_i \,\|\, Wh_k])\big)} .αij​=∑k∈N(i)​exp(LeakyReLU(a⊤[Whi​∥Whk​]))exp(LeakyReLU(a⊤[Whi​∥Whj​]))​.

The graph supplies the mask; attention supplies the weights. This is exactly a transformer restricted to attend only along edges — which is the observation that later motivated graph transformers.

Better than uniform averaging when neighbours differ in relevance, and it does not raise the expressivity ceiling: attention weights are still a function of the neighbourhood multiset.

Designed backwards from the theory. Xu et al. asked what aggregator would be maximally expressive, and derived:

hv(l+1)=MLP⁡(l) ⁣((1+ϵ) hv(l)+∑u∈N(v)hu(l)).h_v^{(l+1)} = \operatorname{MLP}^{(l)}\!\left((1+\epsilon)\,h_v^{(l)} + \sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}(v)} h_u^{(l)}\right).hv(l+1)​=MLP(l)​(1+ϵ)hv(l)​+u∈N(v)∑​hu(l)​​.

Sum, not mean or max. Sum is injective on multisets (given suitable features); mean cannot distinguish {a,b}\{a,b\}{a,b} from {a,a,b,b}\{a,a,b,b\}{a,a,b,b}, and max cannot distinguish {a,b}\{a,b\}{a,b} from {a,a,b}\{a,a,b\}{a,a,b}. The MLP then approximates an arbitrary injective function of that sum.

GIN is provably as powerful as the 1-WL test — the ceiling for message passing — and no more.

Oversmoothing versus oversquashing

Section titled “Oversmoothing versus oversquashing”
OversmoothingOversquashing
Symptomall nodes converge to the same vectordistant information never arrives
Causerepeated averaging is a low-pass filterexponential neighbourhood, fixed-width channel
Shows up onhomophilous graphs, deep stackstasks needing long-range dependencies
Fixesresidual connections, PairNorm, jumping knowledgegraph rewiring, virtual nodes, graph transformers

They are frequently conflated because both say “don’t go deep”. The distinction matters when choosing a fix: residual connections help oversmoothing and do nothing for oversquashing, because the bottleneck is bandwidth, not signal decay.

Check homophily before reaching for a GNN

GNNs assume connected nodes are similar — homophily. On heterophilous graphs (fraud networks, where fraudsters connect to legitimate accounts precisely to blend in), neighbourhood averaging actively destroys the signal. A plain MLP on node features frequently beats a GCN there, and this is a commonly-skipped baseline.

Tier 3 · Formal

The objective, the equations, the guarantees.

The message passing framework

Section titled “The message passing framework”

Gilmer et al.’s formulation, which subsumes essentially every GNN:

mv(l+1)=⨁u∈N(v)Ml(hv(l), hu(l), euv),hv(l+1)=Ul(hv(l), mv(l+1)),m_v^{(l+1)} = \bigoplus_{u \in \mathcal{N}(v)} M_l\big(h_v^{(l)},\, h_u^{(l)},\, e_{uv}\big), \qquad h_v^{(l+1)} = U_l\big(h_v^{(l)},\, m_v^{(l+1)}\big),mv(l+1)​=u∈N(v)⨁​Ml​(hv(l)​,hu(l)​,euv​),hv(l+1)​=Ul​(hv(l)​,mv(l+1)​),

with ⨁\bigoplus⨁ a permutation-invariant aggregator, MlM_lMl​ the message function, and UlU_lUl​ the update function. For graph-level tasks a final readout pools all nodes:

y^=R({hv(L):v∈V}).\hat y = R\big(\{h_v^{(L)} : v \in V\}\big).y^​=R({hv(L)​:v∈V}).

Permutation equivariance is the defining property:

f(PAP⊤, PX)=P f(A,X)f(PAP^{\top},\, PX) = P\,f(A, X)f(PAP⊤,PX)=Pf(A,X)

for any permutation matrix PPP. Relabel the nodes and the outputs permute identically. This is the graph analogue of a CNN’s translation equivariance, and it is why the aggregator must be a set function.

The Weisfeiler-Lehman bound

Section titled “The Weisfeiler-Lehman bound”

The 1-WL colour refinement algorithm:

cv(0)=initial label,cv(l+1)=HASH⁡ ⁣(cv(l), { ⁣{ cu(l):u∈N(v) } ⁣}),c_v^{(0)} = \text{initial label}, \qquad c_v^{(l+1)} = \operatorname{HASH}\!\Big(c_v^{(l)},\ \{\!\{\, c_u^{(l)} : u \in \mathcal{N}(v) \,\}\!\}\Big),cv(0)​=initial label,cv(l+1)​=HASH(cv(l)​, {{cu(l)​:u∈N(v)}}),

where { ⁣{⋅} ⁣}\{\!\{\cdot\}\!\}{{⋅}} denotes a multiset. If two graphs’ colour multisets differ at any round, they are non-isomorphic. If they never differ, the test cannot decide.

Xu et al.’s theorem. Any message-passing GNN maps two graphs to the same representation whenever 1-WL assigns them the same colouring. So:

expressivity(MPNN)  ≤  expressivity(1-WL).\text{expressivity(MPNN)} \;\leq\; \text{expressivity(1-WL)} .expressivity(MPNN)≤expressivity(1-WL).

The proof is a straightforward induction: if cu(l)=cu′(l)c_u^{(l)} = c_{u'}^{(l)}cu(l)​=cu′(l)​ for corresponding nodes at layer lll, then the aggregated multisets are equal, so the updates are equal at layer l+1l+1l+1.

Achieving the bound requires injectivity. GIN attains it because sum over a multiset of countable features is injective, and by universal approximation an MLP can represent the required injective map. Mean fails:

mean⁡({a,b})=mean⁡({a,a,b,b}),\operatorname{mean}(\{a, b\}) = \operatorname{mean}(\{a,a,b,b\}),mean({a,b})=mean({a,a,b,b}),

so any mean-aggregating GNN is strictly weaker than 1-WL — this is the formal reason GCN and GAT sit below GIN.

What lies outside. 1-WL cannot count cycles or triangles, cannot distinguish regular graphs of the same degree, and cannot compute clustering coefficients. Since these are chemically and socially meaningful, the bound is a practical limitation rather than a curiosity. Higher-order kkk-WL variants are strictly more powerful and cost O(nk)O(n^k)O(nk), which is why they are rare in practice.

Oversquashing, formally

Section titled “Oversquashing, formally”

Alon & Yahav’s argument. The number of nodes within kkk hops grows roughly as

∣Nk(v)∣∼dˉ k|\mathcal{N}_k(v)| \sim \bar d^{\,k}∣Nk​(v)∣∼dˉk

for average degree dˉ\bar ddˉ. All of that information must pass through node vvv‘s representation, of fixed size hhh. So the information per source node scales as

hdˉ k,\frac{h}{\bar d^{\,k}},dˉkh​,

decaying exponentially in kkk. A task requiring a specific fact from 5 hops away on a degree-10 graph is trying to route one signal out of 10510^5105 through a fixed-width channel.

The formal measure is the Jacobian sensitivity:

∥∂hv(L)∂hu(0)∥\left\|\frac{\partial h_v^{(L)}}{\partial h_u^{(0)}}\right\|​∂hu(0)​∂hv(L)​​​

for uuu at distance LLL from vvv. Bounds show this decays exponentially in the distance, controlled by the graph’s Ricci curvature at the bottleneck edges — negatively curved edges are the ones that squash. This is why rewiring methods target exactly those edges.

Note the contrast with oversmoothing. Oversmoothing is signal decay; oversquashing is bandwidth. Adding residual connections fixes the first and not the second, because a residual does not widen the channel.

Graph transformers

Section titled “Graph transformers”

Attend over all node pairs rather than only along edges, injecting graph structure through the attention bias instead of the mask:

Aij=(hiWQ)(hjWK)⊤d+bϕ(i,j)+cedge(i,j),A_{ij} = \frac{(h_i W_Q)(h_j W_K)^{\top}}{\sqrt{d}} + b_{\phi(i,j)} + c_{\text{edge}(i,j)},Aij​=d​(hi​WQ​)(hj​WK​)⊤​+bϕ(i,j)​+cedge(i,j)​,

with ϕ(i,j)\phi(i,j)ϕ(i,j) the shortest-path distance and bbb a learned per-distance bias. Graphormer adds a centrality encoding to node features as well.

What this buys. Every node reaches every other in one layer, so oversquashing disappears — there is no bottleneck to squash through. And because shortest-path distance is a stronger signal than the WL colouring, Graphormer provably exceeds 1-WL expressivity.

What it costs. O(n2)O(n^2)O(n2) attention, so it is limited to small graphs — molecules, mostly, where nnn is tens of atoms. It is not an option for a million-node social network, which is why message passing persists.

Tier 4 · Frontier

Open problems, current results, and what is still contested.

Where this stands, as of 2026

Section titled “Where this stands, as of 2026”

Graph transformers dominate molecular property prediction; message passing dominates large-scale graphs. The split is entirely about nnn. For molecules, quadratic attention is affordable and the expressivity gain is real. For web-scale graphs it is not, and sampled message passing remains the only practical option.

AlphaFold is the field’s landmark application, and worth being precise about: it is not a plain GNN. Its Evoformer operates on a pair representation with triangle updates enforcing geometric consistency — closer to a graph transformer with domain-specific structure than to a message-passing network. The lesson generally drawn is that heavy domain structure beats generic architectures on scientific problems.

Positional encodings partially lift the WL ceiling. Laplacian eigenvectors, random-walk encodings, and shortest-path features all inject information message passing cannot derive, raising expressivity without kkk-WL’s cost. This is the most active area and none of the schemes is clearly best.

Benchmarks were bad and are improving. Dwivedi et al. showed that the standard small citation datasets could not distinguish architectures — differences fell inside seed variance. Many pre-2020 GNN comparisons should be read with that in mind. Larger, better-designed benchmarks have since replaced them.

Open problems

Section titled “Open problems”

  • Expressivity beyond 1-WL is expensive. kkk-WL costs O(nk)O(n^k)O(nk). Positional encodings are cheap heuristics without clean guarantees. There is no known method that is both provably more expressive and scalable.

  • Heterophily is under-served. Most architectures assume connected nodes are similar. Fraud detection, adversarial networks, and many biological graphs violate this, and specialised methods do not transfer well between them.

  • Scaling is a systems problem. Neighbour explosion makes minibatching genuinely hard — sampling introduces variance, and full-batch training does not fit. Neither answer is satisfactory.

  • Dynamic graphs are unsolved. Most methods assume a static graph. Real graphs change continuously, and recomputing embeddings on every edge insertion is infeasible.

  • No foundation model for graphs. Unlike text or images, graphs from different domains share no common vocabulary — a molecule and a social network have nothing transferable. Whether a general graph pretraining objective exists is genuinely open.
