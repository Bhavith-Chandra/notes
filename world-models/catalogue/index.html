The World Model Zoo

Thirty-six years of work sits under the phrase “world model”, and the systems it covers have almost nothing in common at the implementation level — a Gaussian process fitted to a cart-pole, a recurrent VAE dreaming Doom, a Monte Carlo tree search over an abstract state with no decoder, a diffusion model rendering playable video at 20 fps. What unifies them is one commitment: learn what happens next, then use it to decide. This page catalogues every major variant, what each predicts and in what space, where each is actually used, and what each gives up.

What you should walk away with

  • Four axes explain the whole zoo. What space you predict in (pixels, continuous latent, discrete tokens, abstract value-equivalent), how the model is used (dream-to-train, search, or plan), what trains it (reconstruction, latent prediction, reward-relevance), and whether dynamics are stochastic.
  • The field oscillated between reconstructing and not reconstructing, twice. Pixels (2015) → latents with decoders (2018) → value-equivalent, no decoder (2019) → tokens (2022) → back to pixels via diffusion (2024). Each swing was a response to a concrete failure of the previous one.
  • MuZero is the sharpest idea here and the least imitated. It never reconstructs anything. Its latent state is defined by predicting reward, value and policy — so it is free to discard everything not decision-relevant. That is a stronger claim than “compress the observation”.
  • “World model” now names two different research programmes. One builds controllers (Dreamer, TD-MPC, MuZero — the model exists to act better). The other builds simulators (Genie, GameNGen, Cosmos — the model exists to generate). They share a name, a lineage, and increasingly little else.
Tier 1 · Intuition

Plain language. No equations, no prerequisites.

The one commitment

Section titled “The one commitment”

Every system on this page learns a function that answers: given where I am and what I do, what happens?

(state, action)⟼next state(\text{state},\ \text{action}) \longmapsto \text{next state}(state, action)⟼next state

That is it. Everything else — the architecture, the loss, the planner — is a choice about how to represent “state”, how to train the map, and what to do with it once you have it.

Once you see that, the zoo organises itself. Two models that look nothing alike are usually making different choices on the same four questions.

The four questions

Section titled “The four questions”

  1. What space do you predict in? Raw pixels are interpretable and expensive and full of irrelevant detail. A continuous latent is compact. Discrete tokens let you reuse language-model machinery. An abstract state with no decoder can throw away everything except what affects decisions.

  2. What do you do with it? Dream: generate synthetic experience and train a policy on it. Search: expand a tree of futures and pick a branch. Plan: optimise an action sequence directly against a goal.

  3. What trains it? Reconstruction (can you rebuild the observation?), latent prediction (can you predict your own encoder’s output?), or value-equivalence (does the model predict reward, value and policy correctly — regardless of whether it resembles the world at all?).

  4. Is it stochastic? Deterministic dynamics are simpler and wrong for anything with genuine uncertainty. Stochastic dynamics are necessary and harder to train.

The question that separates the two research programmes

Ask what happens if the model’s predictions look wrong to a human but the agent acts well. For a controller world model, that is fine — MuZero’s latents are not decodable into game screens and nobody minds. For a simulator world model it is total failure, because the output is the product. These are different goals wearing one name, and confusing them is the most common error in reading this literature.

The taxonomy

Section titled “The taxonomy”

Family 1 — Classical model-based control (1990–2015)

Section titled “Family 1 — Classical model-based control (1990–2015)”

The prehistory, and it is not obsolete. These methods remain the right answer for low-dimensional systems with expensive data.

SystemYearModelKey ideaWhere it still wins
Schmidhuber’s RNN world model1990recurrent netfirst proposal to train a predictive model and backprop through it to a controllerhistorical; the framing is the ancestor of everything here
Dyna1991tabularinterleave real experience with simulated experience from a learned modelthe template for every dream-to-train method since
PILCO2011Gaussian processpropagate model uncertainty analytically through the rolloutcart-pole-scale problems with very few trials; still remarkably sample-efficient
Guided Policy Search2013local linearfit simple local models, use them to supervise a global policyreal robots with limited data
E2C2015locally linear latentlearn a latent where dynamics are linear, so optimal control theory applieswhen you want classical control guarantees on pixel inputs

PILCO deserves specific attention because it did something modern methods mostly abandoned: it modelled its own uncertainty and propagated it. A Gaussian process gives a posterior over dynamics, and PILCO integrates over that posterior when evaluating a policy — so it never exploits a region the model is unsure about.

That is precisely the model exploitation problem that deep world models still have. PILCO solved it, at the cost of scaling to roughly ten state dimensions.

The capability that was traded away

Deep world models scale to pixels and lost calibrated uncertainty. PILCO had calibrated uncertainty and could not scale. Fifteen years on, this trade has not been undone — and it is the single clearest thing the modern literature could recover from the classical one.

Family 2 — Pixel-space video prediction (2015–2019)

Section titled “Family 2 — Pixel-space video prediction (2015–2019)”

Predict the next frame. Interpretable, expensive, and it taught the field what not to do.

SystemYearContributionFailure mode
Oh et al.2015first convincing action-conditional Atari frame predictiondeterministic; blurs under uncertainty
Finn et al.2016predict pixel motion rather than pixel values, for robot pushingstruggles with novel objects
SVG2018learned prior over per-frame latents — genuinely stochastic videostill blurry at long horizons
SimPLe2019full model-based Atari: train PPO entirely inside a learned pixel simulatormodel errors compound; beaten later by latent methods

The lesson these produced. A deterministic model trained with MSE, faced with genuine uncertainty, outputs the mean of the possible futures — which is a blur, not a plausible frame. Every subsequent design either models the stochasticity explicitly (SVG, RSSM) or moves the loss out of pixel space entirely (JEPA), and both moves trace back to this.

Family 3 — Latent dynamics, the Dreamer line (2018–2025)

Section titled “Family 3 — Latent dynamics, the Dreamer line (2018–2025)”

The workhorse family. Encode observations to a compact latent, learn dynamics there, and train a policy on imagined latent rollouts.

  • World Models (2018)
  • PlaNet (2019)
  • Dreamer V1→V3
  • TD-MPC / TD-MPC2

Ha & Schmidhuber. VAE compresses frames to zzz; an MDN-RNN predicts p(zt+1∣zt,at,ht)p(z_{t+1}\mid z_t, a_t, h_t)p(zt+1​∣zt​,at​,ht​); a tiny linear controller (867 parameters) is evolved with CMA-ES on top.

The famous result: training the controller entirely inside the model’s hallucinated dream, then transferring to the real environment. It worked on CarRacing and VizDoom.

The catch, which the paper is honest about: the agent found and exploited the dream’s flaws. Adding a temperature parameter to make the dream noisier — harder, not easier — was needed to prevent it. That is model exploitation, discovered and named in the same paper that popularised the term “world model”.

Use it for: understanding the field’s structure. The V-M-C decomposition is the clearest teaching example. Superseded in practice.

The RSSM. Split the latent into a deterministic recurrent path hth_tht​ and a stochastic path ztz_tzt​, and train with a variational bound plus latent overshooting — a multi-step consistency term forcing the model to be accurate over several steps rather than only one.

No policy at all. PlaNet plans with CEM directly in latent space at every timestep. Pure planning, no learned actor.

Use it for: continuous control from pixels when you want planning rather than a policy, and when re-planning cost is acceptable.

Weakness: CEM at every step is slow, and the horizon is limited by compounding error.

V1 (2019) replaced PlaNet’s CEM with an actor-critic trained on imagined rollouts, and — crucially — backpropagates the value gradient through the learned dynamics. Analytic gradients rather than score-function estimates, which is far lower variance.

V2 (2020) switched the stochastic latent from Gaussian to categorical (32 categories × 32 variables, straight-through gradients). Discrete latents suit the sharp transitions in Atari, where a Gaussian must smear probability across states that cannot occur.

V3 (2023) made it work across 150+ tasks with fixed hyperparameters. The enabling trick is symlog prediction — regressing a symmetric log transform of targets — which stabilises training across wildly different reward and observation scales. First system to collect diamonds in Minecraft from scratch. Published in Nature in 2025.

DayDreamer (2022) applied Dreamer to physical robots, learning locomotion in about an hour of real-world interaction — the strongest practical demonstration that world models buy sample efficiency where it costs real money.

Use it for: the default choice for model-based RL from pixels. Robust, general, well-documented.

The value-equivalent branch of planning. Learn a latent dynamics model with no decoder, trained only to predict reward and value — then plan a short horizon (around 5 steps) and bootstrap with the learned value at the end.

Why this sidesteps compounding error entirely: you never roll out far enough for it to matter. The terminal value carries the long-horizon information instead.

TD-MPC2 scaled this to a single 317M-parameter agent covering 80+ tasks across multiple embodiments and action spaces. Currently among the strongest results in model-based continuous control.

Use it for: continuous control where sample efficiency matters and you want strong performance without Dreamer’s reconstruction cost.

Family 4 — Value-equivalent models, the MuZero line (2019–2022)

Section titled “Family 4 — Value-equivalent models, the MuZero line (2019–2022)”

The most conceptually distinct family, and the one whose central idea is least imitated elsewhere.

SystemYearAddsUse it for
MuZero2019value-equivalent latent + MCTS; matches AlphaZero without knowing the rulesdiscrete-action games and planning problems with a simulator budget
Sampled MuZero2021samples actions instead of enumerating — extends MCTS to continuous and very large action spacescontinuous control, large combinatorial action spaces
EfficientZero2021self-supervised consistency loss, value prefix, off-policy correction; human-level Atari from 2 hours of playextreme sample efficiency on discrete-action tasks
Stochastic MuZero2022chance nodes in the tree, so genuinely stochastic environments are handled correctlybackgammon, 2048, any environment with real randomness

Why value-equivalence is a stronger idea than compression. Every other family trains the latent to represent the observation in some sense. MuZero trains it to predict the quantities that matter for deciding. If the colour of the sky affects nothing, MuZero’s latent is free to discard it entirely — not because a bottleneck forced it, but because nothing in the objective ever asked about it.

The cost. No decoder means no inspectability, no transfer of the model to a new reward function, and a hard dependence on reward signal — you cannot train MuZero on reward-free data.

Family 5 — Token world models (2022–)

Section titled “Family 5 — Token world models (2022–)”

Discretise observations with a VQ-VAE, then model the token sequence with a transformer. Everything built for language models transfers.

SystemYearNotes
Trajectory Transformer2021discretise states, actions and rewards; beam search over the sequence. RL as sequence modelling
IRIS2022VQ-VAE tokens + GPT-style dynamics; strong Atari-100k results, ~1.6 hours of play
TWM2023Transformer-XL dynamics with 100k-interaction budget
STORM2023stochastic transformer world model; better efficiency than IRIS at similar performance
Genie2024learns a latent action space from unlabelled internet video — no action labels at all

Genie is the outlier worth understanding. Every other system here needs (s,a,s′)(s, a, s')(s,a,s′) triples. Genie infers a discrete latent action from consecutive frames via an unsupervised action model, then trains a dynamics model conditioned on it. The result is a controllable, generated 2D world from video that was never labelled with controls — which unlocks internet-scale video as training data for interactive models.

Why tokenisation keeps reappearing

Once your world is a token sequence, you inherit everything: transformers, scaling laws, KV caching, speculative decoding, in-context learning. The same argument drove neural audio codecs and VQ-VAE for images. The cost is quantisation error and a codebook that can collapse.

Family 6 — Diffusion and video world models (2024–)

Section titled “Family 6 — Diffusion and video world models (2024–)”

The return to pixels, for a different purpose.

SystemYearWhat it isPurpose
UniPi2023text-conditioned video generation as a planner; inverse dynamics extracts actionscontroller
DIAMOND2024diffusion world model for Atari; visual detail matters for policy qualitycontroller
GameNGen2024diffusion model runs DOOM at ~20 fps, interactively, with no game enginesimulator
Oasis2024real-time playable Minecraft-like world modelsimulator
Cosmos2025NVIDIA’s world foundation model platform for physical AI and roboticssimulator / data engine
Genie 2 / Genie 32024 / 2025large-scale foundation world models generating playable 3D environmentssimulator

DIAMOND’s finding is the interesting one for controllers. Its argument is that latent world models discard visual detail that turns out to matter — a small sprite that determines a reward, for instance. Diffusion in pixel space preserves it, and the resulting policies are better on exactly those Atari games.

That is a direct challenge to the “reconstruction wastes capacity” thesis, and it is not resolved. The honest reading: which detail matters is task-dependent, and neither family knows in advance which it is discarding.

What a playable Doom does and does not demonstrate

GameNGen generating interactive Doom is a genuine achievement in conditional video generation. It is not evidence that the model understands Doom — there is no explicit game state, no object permanence guarantee, and the paper reports drift over long horizons. A simulator that looks right and has no persistent state is a different thing from a model you could plan in. Read these results as graphics results, which is how their authors largely frame them.

Family 7 — Joint-embedding world models (2023–2026)

Section titled “Family 7 — Joint-embedding world models (2023–2026)”

Predict in embedding space; never reconstruct. The JEPA argument applied to dynamics.

SystemYearContribution
I-JEPA / V-JEPA2023 / 2024representation learning by latent prediction, images then video — the substrate
DINO-WM2024world model on frozen DINOv2 features; zero-shot planning to image goals
Navigation World Models2024conditional video-latent model for navigation; CVPR 2025
PLDM (Sobal et al.)2025planning with latent dynamics learned from reward-free offline data
V-JEPA 22025action-conditioned predictor; zero-shot robot manipulation from a goal image
HWM2026two timescales in one shared latent space; hierarchical planning

DINO-WM’s contribution is architectural and underrated. By freezing a pretrained visual encoder and learning only the dynamics on top, it decouples representation learning from dynamics learning. You can improve either independently, and you get to reuse an encoder trained on far more data than your control task could ever provide.

V-JEPA 2 is the strongest zero-shot result: pretrained on over a million hours of internet video, adapted with fewer than 62 hours of unlabelled robot trajectories, then deployed on Franka arms in unseen labs at roughly 65–80% success on reach, grasp and pick-and-place — with no reward and no task-specific training.

HWM’s result is the starkest number in the literature: flat V-JEPA2-AC planning scores 0% on end-to-end pick-and-place from a single goal image; the hierarchical version scores 70%. See hierarchical planning.

RSSM and the ELBO

Section titled “RSSM and the ELBO”

PlaNet’s model factorises the latent into deterministic hth_tht​ and stochastic sts_tst​:

ht=f(ht−1, st−1, at−1),st∼p(st∣ht),ot∼p(ot∣ht,st).h_t = f\big(h_{t-1},\, s_{t-1},\, a_{t-1}\big), \qquad s_t \sim p\big(s_t \mid h_t\big), \qquad o_t \sim p\big(o_t \mid h_t, s_t\big).ht​=f(ht−1​,st−1​,at−1​),st​∼p(st​∣ht​),ot​∼p(ot​∣ht​,st​).

Training maximises

L=∑t(Eq[log⁡p(ot∣ht,st)]⏟reconstruction  −  β KL(q(st∣o≤t,a<t) ∥ p(st∣ht))⏟prior matching).\mathcal{L} = \sum_{t}\Big(\underbrace{\mathbb{E}_{q}\big[\log p(o_t\mid h_t,s_t)\big]}_{\text{reconstruction}} \;-\; \beta\,\underbrace{\mathrm{KL}\big(q(s_t\mid o_{\leq t}, a_{<t})\,\big\|\,p(s_t\mid h_t)\big)}_{\text{prior matching}}\Big).L=t∑​(reconstructionEq​[logp(ot​∣ht​,st​)]​​−βprior matchingKL(q(st​∣o≤t​,a<t​)​p(st​∣ht​))​​).

Why both paths. The deterministic path is not sampled, so information flows through it without accumulating sampling variance — it can carry a fact from t=0t{=}0t=0 to t=50t{=}50t=50 intact. The stochastic path can represent genuine multimodality. Ablating either measurably degrades performance, which is PlaNet’s core empirical claim.

Latent overshooting. Standard training only enforces one-step accuracy. PlaNet adds a multi-step term:

Lovershoot=∑t∑d=1DKL(q(st∣o≤t) ∥ p(st∣st−d, at−d:t)),\mathcal{L}_{\text{overshoot}} = \sum_{t}\sum_{d=1}^{D} \mathrm{KL}\Big(q(s_t \mid o_{\leq t}) \,\Big\|\, p\big(s_t \mid s_{t-d},\, a_{t-d:t}\big)\Big),Lovershoot​=t∑​d=1∑D​KL(q(st​∣o≤t​)​p(st​∣st−d​,at−d:t​)),

requiring the ddd-step prediction to match the posterior. This directly attacks compounding error by making the model accurate over the horizon it will actually be rolled out for — a small change with a large effect.

DreamerV2’s categorical latent

Section titled “DreamerV2’s categorical latent”

Replace the Gaussian sts_tst​ with 323232 categorical variables of 323232 classes each, sampled with straight-through gradients:

st∼Cat⁡(πθ(ht)),∇θst≈∇θπθ(ht)  (straight-through).s_t \sim \operatorname{Cat}\big(\pi_\theta(h_t)\big), \qquad \nabla_\theta s_t \approx \nabla_\theta \pi_\theta(h_t)\ \ (\text{straight-through}).st​∼Cat(πθ​(ht​)),∇θ​st​≈∇θ​πθ​(ht​)  (straight-through).

Why discrete helps on Atari. A Gaussian posterior must place probability mass continuously. When the true next state is one of a few sharply distinct possibilities — a life lost or not, a level transition or not — a Gaussian smears mass across intermediate states that cannot occur. Categorical variables represent exactly the multimodality present.

This is the same argument that motivates VQ-VAE over VAE for images, and the same straight-through estimator.

Dreamer’s analytic value gradient

Section titled “Dreamer’s analytic value gradient”

Dreamer’s advantage over PlaNet is the objective it optimises. Imagine a rollout {s^τ}\{\hat s_\tau\}{s^τ​} under πθ\pi_\thetaπθ​ and maximise

max⁡θ Epϕ,πθ[∑τ=tt+Hγτ−tVλ(s^τ)],\max_\theta\ \mathbb{E}_{p_\phi,\pi_\theta}\left[\sum_{\tau=t}^{t+H}\gamma^{\tau - t} V_\lambda(\hat s_\tau)\right],θmax​ Epϕ​,πθ​​[τ=t∑t+H​γτ−tVλ​(s^τ​)],

with VλV_\lambdaVλ​ a TD(λ\lambdaλ) value estimate. Because pϕp_\phipϕ​ is a differentiable neural network, the gradient can be taken through the dynamics:

∇θV  =  ∂V∂s^τ+1⋅∂pϕ(s^τ,aτ)∂aτ⋅∂πθ(s^τ)∂θ.\nabla_\theta V \;=\; \frac{\partial V}{\partial \hat s_{\tau+1}}\cdot\frac{\partial p_\phi(\hat s_\tau, a_\tau)}{\partial a_\tau}\cdot\frac{\partial \pi_\theta(\hat s_\tau)}{\partial \theta} .∇θ​V=∂s^τ+1​∂V​⋅∂aτ​∂pϕ​(s^τ​,aτ​)​⋅∂θ∂πθ​(s^τ​)​.

Compare with the score-function estimator used in model-free policy gradients, which multiplies ∇log⁡π\nabla\log\pi∇logπ by a scalar return and carries no information about how the return would change. The analytic path is dramatically lower variance, and it is the main reason model-based RL is more sample-efficient — not the imagined data volume, which is the usual explanation.

MuZero’s value equivalence

Section titled “MuZero’s value equivalence”

Three networks:

s0=hθ(o1,…,ot),(sk,r^k)=gθ(sk−1,ak),(p^k,v^k)=fθ(sk).s^0 = h_\theta(o_1,\dots,o_t), \qquad (s^{k}, \hat r^{k}) = g_\theta(s^{k-1}, a^{k}), \qquad (\hat p^{k}, \hat v^{k}) = f_\theta(s^{k}).s0=hθ​(o1​,…,ot​),(sk,r^k)=gθ​(sk−1,ak),(p^​k,v^k)=fθ​(sk).

Trained by unrolling KKK steps and matching three observable targets:

L=∑k=0K[ℓr(ut+k, r^k)⏟reward+ℓv(zt+k, v^k)⏟value+ℓp(πt+k, p^k)⏟policy]+c∥θ∥2,\mathcal{L} = \sum_{k=0}^{K}\Big[\underbrace{\ell^{r}\big(u_{t+k},\,\hat r^{k}\big)}_{\text{reward}} + \underbrace{\ell^{v}\big(z_{t+k},\,\hat v^{k}\big)}_{\text{value}} + \underbrace{\ell^{p}\big(\pi_{t+k},\,\hat p^{k}\big)}_{\text{policy}}\Big] + c\|\theta\|^2 ,L=k=0∑K​[rewardℓr(ut+k​,r^k)​​+valueℓv(zt+k​,v^k)​​+policyℓp(πt+k​,p^​k)​​]+c∥θ∥2,

with πt+k\pi_{t+k}πt+k​ the MCTS visit-count distribution and zt+kz_{t+k}zt+k​ the bootstrapped nnn-step return.

The value-equivalence principle, stated precisely. Two models M1,M2M_1, M_2M1​,M2​ are value-equivalent with respect to a policy set Π\PiΠ and function set V\mathcal{V}V if

TM1πv=TM2πv∀π∈Π, v∈V,\mathcal{T}^{\pi}_{M_1} v = \mathcal{T}^{\pi}_{M_2} v \quad \forall \pi\in\Pi,\ v\in\mathcal{V},TM1​π​v=TM2​π​v∀π∈Π, v∈V,

where Tπ\mathcal{T}^\piTπ is the Bellman operator. MuZero seeks a model that is value-equivalent to the real environment — not one that resembles it. A model can be arbitrarily unlike reality internally and still induce identical optimal behaviour, and searching over value-equivalent models is a strictly easier problem than searching over accurate ones.

This is the deepest idea in the catalogue, and note what it costs: the latent is defined relative to a reward function, so it does not transfer to a new task.

MCTS with a learned model

Section titled “MCTS with a learned model”

Selection uses pUCT:

ak=arg⁡max⁡a[Q(s,a)+P(s,a)⋅∑bN(s,b)1+N(s,a)(c1+log⁡∑bN(s,b)+c2+1c2)].a^{k} = \arg\max_{a}\left[ Q(s,a) + P(s,a)\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\textstyle\sum_b N(s,b)}}{1+N(s,a)}\left(c_1 + \log\frac{\sum_b N(s,b) + c_2 + 1}{c_2}\right)\right].ak=argamax​[Q(s,a)+P(s,a)⋅1+N(s,a)∑b​N(s,b)​​(c1​+logc2​∑b​N(s,b)+c2​+1​)].

The first term exploits; the second explores, weighted by the prior PPP and decayed as the visit count grows. Because gθg_\thetagθ​ is a network rather than a simulator, each expansion is a forward pass — so tree depth trades directly against wall-clock in a way it does not for a real simulator.

Stochastic MuZero adds chance nodes: after an action node, an afterstate leads to a chance node whose children are sampled outcomes with learned probabilities. Without this, MuZero in a stochastic environment learns an expectation model, and planning through expectations is wrong — the mean of the outcomes is not an outcome.

Diffusion world models

Section titled “Diffusion world models”

DIAMOND models p(ot+1∣o≤t,a≤t)p(o_{t+1}\mid o_{\leq t}, a_{\leq t})p(ot+1​∣o≤t​,a≤t​) with a diffusion process conditioned on history:

ot+1=Denoise⁡θ(ϵ, ot−L:t, at−L:t).o_{t+1} = \operatorname{Denoise}_\theta\big(\epsilon,\ o_{t-L:t},\ a_{t-L:t}\big).ot+1​=Denoiseθ​(ϵ, ot−L:t​, at−L:t​).

Why diffusion rather than a deterministic decoder. A deterministic model trained with MSE outputs the conditional mean, which for a multimodal future is a blur that is not a valid frame. Diffusion samples from the conditional distribution, so it produces a particular plausible future. The 2015-era blurring problem, solved properly.

The cost is sampling. Even few-step samplers make each imagined timestep far more expensive than an RSSM’s single forward pass, which caps rollout length and therefore planning horizon.

Where this sits between the other families. Reconstruction sets a floor on I(Z;X)I(Z;X)I(Z;X) and wastes capacity on irrelevant detail. Where the field is, as of 2026

Section titled “Where the field is, as of 2026”

The controller/simulator split is now the main structural fact. Genie 3, GameNGen and Cosmos are generative video systems that happen to be interactive. V-JEPA 2, TD-MPC2 and HWM are control systems that happen to predict. Both call themselves world models; the benchmarks, the failure modes, and the research communities have largely separated.

Zero-shot planning from goal images works, at moderate reliability. Roughly 65–80% on real manipulation in unseen environments with no task-specific training. Real, and not deployable.

Hierarchy is the settled answer to long horizons. Flat planners fail totally (0%) where hierarchical ones succeed (70%), at ~3× lower planning cost, and the gain holds across several different underlying world models — which suggests it is the planning structure, not the representation, doing the work.

Reconstruct-versus-predict is genuinely unresolved. DIAMOND argues visual detail matters and demonstrates it on specific Atari games. V-JEPA argues detail wastes capacity and demonstrates it on transfer. Both are right about their evidence. Nobody can say in advance which detail your task needs.

Open problems, across all families

Section titled “Open problems, across all families”

  • Uncertainty is unmodelled. PILCO had it in 2011 and nothing scalable has it now. Without it, planners cannot distinguish a good plan from an exploit of a model error — the single largest gap between current systems and reliable ones.

  • No transfer between world models. Two models of the same environment, differently trained, have incomparable latent spaces. This blocks modular reuse and forces hierarchical systems to train levels jointly.

  • Evaluation is weak and inconsistent. Success rate on a handful of tabletop tasks, with benchmark suites differing enough between papers that cross-paper comparison is unreliable. Be sceptical of leaderboard claims in this subfield specifically.

  • Long-horizon consistency. Generative world models drift — objects vanish, layouts change. There is no persistent state, so nothing enforces object permanence.

  • Compositional generalisation is absent. Every system plans within the distribution of dynamics it saw. Reasoning about a novel object with novel physics remains out of reach for all of them.

Reading path

Section titled “Reading path”

If you read four papers

Ha & Schmidhuber (2018) for the framing and the V-M-C decomposition. PlaNet (2019) for the RSSM ablations — the most instructive pages in the literature on why latents split into deterministic and stochastic parts. MuZero (2019) for value equivalence, the deepest idea here. V-JEPA 2 (2025) for the current zero-shot frontier.

Then, by interest: DreamerV3 for the general-purpose controller, TD-MPC2 for continuous control, Genie for learned action spaces, DIAMOND for the counter-argument to latent prediction, and HWM for hierarchy.
