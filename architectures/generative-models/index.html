The Generative Model Zoo

Every generative model answers the same question — what does the data distribution look like, and can I draw from it? — and every one of them makes a trade among three things you would like at once: exact likelihoods, high sample quality, and fast sampling. No family gets all three. That constraint, not architectural fashion, explains why the field cycled through GANs, flows and diffusion, and why autoregressive models never went away.

What you should walk away with

  • The generative trilemma organises everything. Fast sampling, high quality, tractable likelihood — pick two. GANs sacrifice likelihood, flows sacrifice quality, diffusion sacrificed speed until distillation, autoregressive models sacrifice speed permanently.
  • The likelihood/quality gap is not a bug in either metric. A model can have excellent likelihood and produce terrible samples, and vice versa. Likelihood is dominated by how well you cover the tails; perceptual quality is dominated by the mode you land in.
  • GANs are not dead, they are specialised. One-step sampling remains unmatched, and for real-time or on-device generation that still decides it. What killed them for general use was training instability and mode collapse, not sample quality.
  • Diffusion won by decomposing the hard problem. A GAN learns one violent jump from noise to image against an adversary. Diffusion learns a thousand tiny denoising steps, each an ordinary regression — stable, scalable, and slow. Every recent advance attacks the slowness.

What all of them are doing

Section titled “What all of them are doing”

You have a pile of photographs. Somewhere out there is a probability distribution pdatap_{\text{data}}pdata​ that made them. You want two things from a model of it:

  • Sample. Produce new photographs that could plausibly have come from the same pile.
  • Score. Say how likely a given photograph is under the distribution.

Some families do both. Some do only the first, and cannot tell you how likely anything is. Which one you need decides most of your choice.

Why likelihood and beauty come apart

Section titled “Why likelihood and beauty come apart”

This confuses people, and the explanation is worth having.

Likelihood is an average of log⁡p(x)\log p(x)logp(x) over held-out data. It is dominated by the worst cases — a model that assigns near-zero probability to a few real images takes an enormous penalty, because log⁡0=−∞\log 0 = -\inftylog0=−∞. So a good likelihood forces you to cover everything, including the strange tails.

Perceptual quality is judged on what you produce. A model that generates only the most typical, safest images looks excellent and has ignored most of the distribution.

So the two metrics reward opposite behaviours. A model that covers everything badly beats a model that covers a little superbly on likelihood, and loses on FID. Neither metric is wrong; they measure different things, and reporting only one is how a great deal of generative-modelling progress got overstated.

The one-line diagnosis

Blurry outputs mean the model is mode-covering — spreading probability over everything, which is what maximum likelihood asks for. Sharp but repetitive outputs mean it is mode-seeking — committing to a few modes, which is what an adversarial loss encourages. This is the same forward/reverse KL asymmetry from probability, showing up as a visible artefact.

Two networks in a game. A generator maps noise to images; a discriminator tries to tell real from fake. The generator’s loss is the discriminator’s failure.

What it gives you. One-step sampling — a single forward pass — and, at its peak, the best-looking images anyone had produced. StyleGAN2 faces were the state of the art for years.

What it costs. No likelihood. Ever. The model never represents p(x)p(x)p(x); it only produces samples, which makes it an implicit model. You cannot score, cannot detect out-of-distribution inputs, cannot compress.

The failure modes, both structural:

  • Mode collapse. The generator finds one output that fools the discriminator and produces only that. It is winning the game as defined, and the game was defined wrong.
  • Non-convergence. This is a minimax problem, not a minimisation. There is no loss that decreases monotonically, so you cannot tell from the curves whether training is going well — you have to look at samples.

The fixes that mattered. WGAN replaced the Jensen–Shannon objective with an Earth-Mover distance that gives useful gradients even when the distributions do not overlap. WGAN-GP replaced weight clipping with a gradient penalty. Spectral normalisation constrains the discriminator’s Lipschitz constant cheaply and became standard.

Use it today for: real-time and on-device generation, super-resolution, image-to-image translation (pix2pix, CycleGAN), and as the perceptual loss inside neural audio codecs and VQ-GAN-style tokenisers — which is where most GAN training now actually happens.

Encode to a distribution, decode from a sample, regularise toward a prior. Covered fully on Neural Encoders.

What it gives you. A smooth, samplable latent space; fast one-pass sampling; a bound on likelihood; and a genuinely useful encoder — the only family here where inference is built in.

What it costs. Blur. The reconstruction term is typically Gaussian, so the decoder outputs a conditional mean, and the mean of several plausible images is not an image. Plus posterior collapse when the decoder is strong enough to ignore the latent.

Its real legacy is as a component. VQ-VAE tokenisers feed diffusion and audio models; latent diffusion runs inside a VAE’s latent space. The VAE mostly stopped being an endpoint and became infrastructure.

Build the distribution from an invertible chain. Start with a simple distribution, apply a sequence of invertible transformations, and use the change-of-variables formula to get an exact density.

What it gives you. Exact likelihood — not a bound, the actual number — plus fast sampling and exact inference. Uniquely among these families, all three of those at once.

What it costs. The transformations must be invertible with a tractable Jacobian determinant, and that constraint is severe. Every layer must preserve dimensionality — no bottleneck, ever — and the architectures you are allowed (coupling layers, autoregressive flows) are far less expressive per parameter than an unconstrained network.

The result: flows have never matched GAN or diffusion sample quality on images at comparable compute.

Use it for: density estimation proper, out-of-distribution detection, variational inference (as flexible posteriors), simulation-based inference in the sciences, and lossless compression. Anywhere the number matters more than the picture.

Continuous flows (Neural ODE, FFJORD) replace the discrete chain with an ODE, trading the architectural constraint for numerical integration cost — and they are the direct ancestor of flow matching.

Factorise the joint into a product of conditionals and model each one. For images, predict pixel iii given pixels 1..i−11..i-11..i−1; for text, the same over tokens.

What it gives you. Exact likelihood, stable maximum-likelihood training with no adversary, and — as language models demonstrated — the cleanest scaling behaviour of any family here.

What it costs. Sampling is inherently sequential: nnn forward passes for nnn elements. For a 1024×1024 image that is a million sequential steps, which is why autoregressive image models lost to diffusion despite better likelihoods.

Where the ordering bites. Text has a natural order. Images do not — raster order forces the model to predict a pixel from the ones above and to the left, which is an arbitrary and unnatural conditioning structure. PixelCNN’s masked convolutions have a blind spot for exactly this reason.

Use it for: anything with a natural sequential order. Which is to say: all of language modelling, and — via neural codecs — audio. It never went away; it moved domains.

Destroy data with noise, learn to reverse it. Covered in full on Diffusion Models.

Why it won. It decomposed an impossible problem into a thousand easy ones. Each denoising step is an ordinary supervised regression with a squared loss — no adversary, no equilibrium, no invertibility constraint. Training is about as stable as training a classifier, and it scales.

What it costs. Sampling. Hundreds of network evaluations per image at first; DDIM, distillation, consistency models and rectified flow have pushed that toward one, each by adding machinery.

Use it for: essentially all high-quality image, video and audio generation today, plus as a policy class in robotics and as a world model.

Define an energy Eθ(x)E_\theta(x)Eθ​(x) and let p(x)∝e−E(x)p(x) \propto e^{-E(x)}p(x)∝e−E(x). Maximum flexibility — any scalar function of xxx defines a valid distribution.

What it costs, and it is fatal for most uses. The normalising constant Z=∫e−E(x)dxZ = \int e^{-E(x)}dxZ=∫e−E(x)dx is intractable. You cannot compute the likelihood, and training by maximum likelihood requires sampling from the model itself at every step, usually via MCMC, which is slow and unreliable in high dimensions.

Why it still matters. The score function ∇xlog⁡p(x)=−∇xE(x)\nabla_x \log p(x) = -\nabla_x E(x)∇x​logp(x)=−∇x​E(x) does not involve ZZZ at all — the gradient of the log-density is computable even when the density is not. That single observation is the bridge to score matching and therefore to diffusion. Modern diffusion models are, in a precise sense, the energy-based programme made trainable.

FID is the field's most over-trusted number

FID compares Inception-v3 feature statistics between real and generated sets. It is biased by sample count (fewer samples inflates it), sensitive to image preprocessing and JPEG artefacts, and inherits whatever ImageNet classification happened to make salient. It also collapses two distinct failures — poor fidelity and poor diversity — into one scalar, so a mode-collapsed model and a blurry one can score alike. Report precision and recall separately if you want to know which is happening.

Autoregressive: exact factorisation

Section titled “Autoregressive: exact factorisation”
p(x)=∏i=1np(xi∣x<i),L=−∑ilog⁡pθ(xi∣x<i).p(x) = \prod_{i=1}^{n} p\big(x_i \mid x_{<i}\big), \qquad \mathcal{L} = -\sum_{i} \log p_\theta\big(x_i\mid x_{<i}\big).p(x)=i=1∏n​p(xi​∣x<i​),L=−i∑​logpθ​(xi​∣x<i​).

Exact by the chain rule of probability — no approximation anywhere, which is why the likelihood is exact and training is stable.

The cost is structural. Sampling requires nnn sequential evaluations, and no amount of hardware parallelism removes a sequential dependency. Training is parallel (teacher forcing evaluates all conditionals at once), which produces the characteristic asymmetry: fast to train, slow to sample — the exact reverse of diffusion.

Discretised logistic mixture. PixelCNN++‘s practically important detail: modelling a 256-way softmax per channel wastes capacity, because pixel values 127 and 128 are nearly the same and a softmax treats them as unrelated categories. Instead model a continuous mixture and integrate over each discrete bin:

which encodes ordinality, uses far fewer parameters, and improves likelihood measurably.

Why maximum likelihood produces blur

Section titled “Why maximum likelihood produces blur”

The MLE objective is equivalent to minimising forward KL:

arg⁡max⁡θEpdata[log⁡pθ(x)]=arg⁡min⁡θ KL(pdata ∥ pθ).\arg\max_\theta \mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}\big[\log p_\theta(x)\big] = \arg\min_\theta\ \mathrm{KL}\big(p_{\text{data}}\,\|\,p_\theta\big).argθmax​Epdata​​[logpθ​(x)]=argθmin​ KL(pdata​∥pθ​).

Forward KL is Epdata[log⁡pdata/pθ]\mathbb{E}_{p_{\text{data}}}[\log p_{\text{data}}/p_\theta]Epdata​​[logpdata​/pθ​], which is infinite wherever pdata>0p_{\text{data}} > 0pdata​>0 and pθ=0p_\theta = 0pθ​=0. The penalty for missing a mode is unbounded; the penalty for putting mass where there is no data is merely finite.

So MLE is mode-covering: it spreads probability to cover everything, including the space between modes. For a unimodal decoder that means outputting an average, and an average of images is a blur.

An adversarial loss approximates something closer to reverse KL, which is mode-seeking — it is punished for generating implausible samples and not for missing modes. Hence sharp, and hence mode collapse.

These are two failure modes of one asymmetry, and no choice of architecture escapes it — only a choice of divergence does.

Tier 4 · Frontier

Open problems, current results, and what is still contested.

Where this stands, as of 2026

Section titled “Where this stands, as of 2026”

Diffusion and flow matching own high-quality generation, and the two have largely merged notationally — diffusion with a particular schedule is flow matching along a curved path. New work is written in flow-matching terms because the formulation is simpler and the straight-path property gives a principled route to few-step sampling.

Autoregressive owns everything with a natural order. Language, and via tokenisation, audio and increasingly video. The scaling behaviour is cleaner than any other family’s, and that has proven decisive at frontier scale.

GANs survive in a well-defined niche. One-step sampling is still unmatched where latency is hard-constrained. And GAN losses live on inside other systems — the discriminator in a neural audio codec or a VQ-GAN tokeniser is doing perceptual work that no reconstruction loss does.

Flows are healthy in the sciences and marginal in vision. Simulation-based inference, lattice field theory sampling, and molecular conformer generation all use flows precisely because the exact likelihood is the deliverable. Nobody is trying to beat diffusion on faces with them any more, and that is a sensible division.

The likelihood/quality gap is now understood rather than argued about. Papers report FID and precision/recall and likelihood where applicable, because the field accepted that one number cannot capture both fidelity and coverage.

Open problems

Section titled “Open problems”

  • Evaluation. FID’s flaws are documented and it remains the default because the alternatives are worse or more expensive. Human evaluation is the ground truth and does not scale.

  • One-step generation without a second stage. Distillation and consistency models work and require training a teacher first. Nobody has a single-stage method that samples in one step at diffusion quality.

  • Discrete diffusion is unresolved. The Gaussian machinery does not transfer to tokens, several corruption processes have been proposed, and none clearly wins — which matters because it is the main route to non-autoregressive text generation.

  • Memorisation. Diffusion models provably reproduce training examples under some conditions, particularly for duplicated data. The relationship between duplication, capacity and extraction risk is only partly mapped, and it has direct legal consequences.

  • Controllability. Classifier-free guidance is essential, unprincipled, and samples from a distribution nobody characterised. See Diffusion.

Choosing one

Section titled “Choosing one”

A decision procedure

Need an exact likelihood? Flow (continuous data) or autoregressive (discrete). Nothing else gives you the number.

Need one-step sampling under a hard latency budget? GAN, or a distilled diffusion model if you can afford the extra training stage.

Need the best possible images, video or audio? Diffusion or flow matching. Not close.

Generating something with a natural order? Autoregressive. This is why language models are what they are.

Need an encoder as well as a generator? VAE or flow — the only families with built-in inference.

Doing science where the density is the deliverable? Flow. The invertibility you pay for is exactly what you are buying.
