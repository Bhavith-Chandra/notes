Diffusion Models

Take an image and add noise until it is indistinguishable from static. That direction is trivial. Now learn to undo one step of it — that is a small, well-posed regression problem — and chain the learned inverse a thousand times to turn static into an image. The genius is not in the model; it is in having decomposed an impossibly hard generative problem into a sequence of easy denoising problems.

What you should walk away with

  • The hard problem was split, not solved. Mapping noise to images in one shot is what GANs attempted, and it is unstable. Diffusion asks for a thousand tiny corrections instead, each of which is an ordinary supervised regression.
  • The network predicts the noise, and that turns out to be the score. Up to a scale factor, predicting ϵ\epsilonϵ is estimating ∇xlog⁡p(x)\nabla_x \log p(x)∇x​logp(x) — the direction of increasing data density. The denoiser and the score function are the same object seen two ways.
  • Sampling cost is the defining weakness, and every recent advance attacks it. DDIM, distillation, consistency models, and rectified flow all exist to reduce a thousand network evaluations toward one.
  • Guidance is a knob that trades diversity for fidelity, and it is not principled. Classifier-free guidance extrapolates beyond the conditional prediction. It works spectacularly and it is sampling from a distribution nobody trained.
Tier 1 · Intuition

Plain language. No equations, no prerequisites.

Sculpting from static

Section titled “Sculpting from static”

Imagine a photograph slowly dissolving into television static — a bit more noise each second, until after a thousand seconds nothing remains but grain. Filming that is easy; you just add noise.

Now play the film backwards. Static resolves into an image. That reversed film is exactly what a diffusion model produces, and the way you get it is almost absurdly direct: show a network single frames of the forward film and ask it to guess what the previous frame looked like.

Each individual guess is easy. The frame at t=500t=500t=500 and the frame at t=499t=499t=499 are nearly identical — the difference is one small step of noise. A network can learn that. Chain five hundred such small corrections together, starting from pure static, and you have generated an image.

Why splitting the problem is the whole idea

Section titled “Why splitting the problem is the whole idea”

A GAN tries to jump from a random vector to a finished image in one forward pass. That is a violently hard function to learn, and the training procedure — a minimax game against a discriminator — is notoriously unstable, prone to mode collapse, and hard to scale.

Diffusion refuses the jump. It asks a thousand times for a barely-perceptible improvement, and each request is a plain supervised regression with a squared error loss. There is no adversary, no game, no equilibrium to find. Training is about as stable as training a classifier.

The trade you are making

You have bought training stability with sampling cost. A GAN generates in one network evaluation; a diffusion model classically needs hundreds or thousands. Everything interesting in the last three years of this field is an attempt to get the stability without paying the full sampling bill.

What “predict the noise” really buys you

Section titled “What “predict the noise” really buys you”

The network is not asked “what does the clean image look like?” It is asked “what noise was added?” — which sounds like a strange choice and is the crucial one.

The answer is that the added noise points away from the data. Subtracting it points toward the data. So a network trained to identify noise has implicitly learned, at every point in image space, which direction leads toward plausible-looking images. That direction field is called the score, and having it is equivalent to knowing the shape of the data distribution.

This is why the same model that denoises can also generate: generation is just repeatedly stepping in the direction the score points.

Tier 2 · Mechanics

How it actually works. Pseudocode, no heavy maths.

The two processes

Section titled “The two processes”

Training, which is simpler than you expect

Section titled “Training, which is simpler than you expect”

  1. Sample a clean example x0x_0x0​ from the dataset and a random timestep t∼U{1,…,T}t \sim \mathcal{U}\{1,\dots,T\}t∼U{1,…,T}.

  2. Corrupt in one shot. Draw ϵ∼N(0,I)\epsilon \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I)ϵ∼N(0,I) and form xt=αˉt x0+1−αˉt ϵx_t = \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}\,x_0 + \sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}\,\epsilonxt​=αˉt​​x0​+1−αˉt​​ϵ. No simulation — the closed form is why this is cheap.

  3. Predict. Feed xtx_txt​ and ttt to the network; it outputs ϵθ(xt,t)\epsilon_\theta(x_t, t)ϵθ​(xt​,t).

  4. Regress. Loss is ∥ϵ−ϵθ(xt,t)∥2\|\epsilon - \epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)\|^2∥ϵ−ϵθ​(xt​,t)∥2. That is the whole objective.

Three lines of actual work. There is no adversary, no sampling during training, and no sequential rollout — every step is independent, so training parallelises perfectly.

The asymmetry that catches people out

Training is embarrassingly parallel and cheap per step. Sampling is inherently sequential and expensive. A diffusion model is unusual in that its inference cost dwarfs its per-step training cost, which inverts the usual intuitions about where to optimise.

Sampling, and how to make it faster

Section titled “Sampling, and how to make it faster”

The naive sampler (DDPM ancestral sampling) walks back through all TTT steps, adding a little fresh noise at each. Quality is good; 1000 network evaluations per image is not.

  • DDIM
  • Guidance
  • Latent diffusion
  • Architecture

DDIM’s observation: the DDPM training objective does not actually pin down the sampler. There is a family of reverse processes with the same marginals, and one member of that family is deterministic.

Removing the injected noise turns sampling into solving an ODE rather than an SDE. Two consequences follow immediately:

  • You can take bigger steps. ODE solvers tolerate coarse discretisation far better. 20–50 steps often suffice instead of 1000.
  • The mapping becomes invertible. The same latent always produces the same image, so you can encode a real image to its latent and edit it — the basis of most image-editing pipelines.

No retraining is needed. DDIM is a different way of sampling an already-trained DDPM.

Conditional generation (“a photo of a cat”) works, but early samples were often only loosely faithful to the prompt.

Classifier guidance used the gradient of a separately-trained noise-robust classifier to push samples toward the class. It works and requires an extra model trained on noisy inputs, which is a nuisance.

Classifier-free guidance removed the classifier entirely. Train one model on both conditional and unconditional prediction (drop the condition ~10% of the time), then at sampling extrapolate:

ϵ~=ϵθ(xt,∅)+w[ϵθ(xt,c)−ϵθ(xt,∅)].\tilde\epsilon = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\big[\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\big].ϵ~=ϵθ​(xt​,∅)+w[ϵθ​(xt​,c)−ϵθ​(xt​,∅)].

At w=1w=1w=1 this is ordinary conditional sampling. At w>1w > 1w>1 you are moving past the conditional prediction, away from the unconditional one — exaggerating whatever the condition contributes.

This is a hack and it is the single most important sampling technique in the field. Every text-to-image system uses it, typically at w≈7w \approx 7w≈7. Prompt adherence improves dramatically, diversity drops, and at high www you get saturated, over-contrasted images. There is no clean theory saying what distribution you are sampling from.

Pixel-space diffusion at 1024×1024 is brutally expensive — the network processes a million-dimensional tensor a thousand times per image.

Latent diffusion runs the whole process in the latent space of a pretrained autoencoder instead. Encode to (say) 64×64×4, diffuse there, decode once at the end. That is roughly a 48× reduction in the dimensionality the diffusion model sees.

The reason it works without visible quality loss is a division of labour: the autoencoder handles perceptual detail (texture, high-frequency content) and the diffusion model handles semantic structure (what is in the image, where). These turn out to be largely separable, and this insight is what made Stable Diffusion runnable on consumer hardware.

The original backbone was a U-Net — convolutional, with skip connections between matching resolutions, and attention layers at low resolutions.

DiT (Peebles & Xie, 2022) replaced it with a plain transformer on latent patches, and showed the U-Net’s inductive biases are not necessary. More importantly, DiT scales predictably: generation quality improves smoothly with model FLOPs, which the U-Net variants did not do as cleanly.

This mattered because it let diffusion inherit the entire transformer scaling toolkit. Most large modern image and video generators are transformer-based.

The score connection

Section titled “The score connection”

For the Gaussian perturbation kernel,

∇xtlog⁡q(xt∣x0)=−xt−αˉtx01−αˉt=−ϵ1−αˉt.\nabla_{x_t} \log q(x_t \mid x_0) = -\frac{x_t - \sqrt{\bar\alpha_t}x_0}{1-\bar\alpha_t} = -\frac{\epsilon}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}} .∇xt​​logq(xt​∣x0​)=−1−αˉt​xt​−αˉt​​x0​​=−1−αˉt​​ϵ​.

So

sθ(xt,t)  ≡  ∇xtlog⁡pt(xt)  ≈  −ϵθ(xt,t)1−αˉt.s_\theta(x_t, t) \;\equiv\; \nabla_{x_t}\log p_t(x_t) \;\approx\; -\frac{\epsilon_\theta(x_t,t)}{\sqrt{1-\bar\alpha_t}} .sθ​(xt​,t)≡∇xt​​logpt​(xt​)≈−1−αˉt​​ϵθ​(xt​,t)​.

Noise prediction and score estimation are the same task up to a scale. This identity unified two literatures that had developed separately — Sohl-Dickstein’s thermodynamic framing and Song & Ermon’s score matching — and is why the modern account is usually given in score language.

Song et al. then showed both are discretisations of a continuous-time SDE:

dx=f(x,t) dt+g(t) dw(forward),dx = f(x,t)\,dt + g(t)\,dw \qquad\text{(forward)},dx=f(x,t)dt+g(t)dw(forward), dx=[f(x,t)−g(t)2∇xlog⁡pt(x)]dt+g(t) dwˉ(reverse).dx = \big[f(x,t) - g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x)\big]dt + g(t)\,d\bar w \qquad\text{(reverse)}.dx=[f(x,t)−g(t)2∇x​logpt​(x)]dt+g(t)dwˉ(reverse).

And every SDE has an associated probability flow ODE with identical marginals:

dxdt=f(x,t)−12g(t)2∇xlog⁡pt(x).\frac{dx}{dt} = f(x,t) - \tfrac{1}{2}g(t)^2 \nabla_x \log p_t(x).dtdx​=f(x,t)−21​g(t)2∇x​logpt​(x).

Deterministic sampling — DDIM — is exactly a discretisation of this ODE. That recontextualises DDIM from “a clever trick” to “the natural deterministic member of the family”, and it opens the door to using any off-the-shelf ODE solver, which is where the fast samplers (DPM-Solver and relatives) come from.

Flow matching

Section titled “Flow matching”

The framing that is displacing diffusion notation. Instead of a noising process, posit a straight-line probability path between noise and data:

xt=(1−t) x0+t x1,x0∼N(0,I), x1∼pdata, t∈[0,1].x_t = (1-t)\,x_0 + t\,x_1, \qquad x_0 \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I),\ x_1 \sim p_{\text{data}},\ t \in [0,1].xt​=(1−t)x0​+tx1​,x0​∼N(0,I), x1​∼pdata​, t∈[0,1].

The velocity field along this path is trivially ut=x1−x0u_t = x_1 - x_0ut​=x1​−x0​, and you regress a network onto it:

LFM=Et,x0,x1[∥vθ(xt,t)−(x1−x0)∥2].\mathcal{L}_{\text{FM}} = \mathbb{E}_{t, x_0, x_1}\Big[\big\|v_\theta(x_t, t) - (x_1 - x_0)\big\|^2\Big].LFM​=Et,x0​,x1​​[​vθ​(xt​,t)−(x1​−x0​)​2].

Compare with Lsimple\mathcal{L}_{\text{simple}}Lsimple​: structurally identical, conceptually much simpler. No noise schedule, no αˉt\bar\alpha_tαˉt​, no variational bound — just “learn the velocity field of a straight-line interpolation”.

Why straightness matters. ODE integration error depends on path curvature. A perfectly straight path can be integrated exactly in one Euler step. Diffusion’s implied paths are curved, which is precisely why they need many steps. Rectified flow makes this explicit: iteratively re-couple samples to straighten the paths, and each round of straightening buys you fewer sampling steps.

How to hold the relationship

Flow matching is not a rival to diffusion so much as a cleaner coordinate system for the same object. Diffusion with a particular schedule is a flow-matching model with a particular (curved) path. The practical argument for flow matching is that the straight path is both simpler to state and faster to sample — and that argument has largely won on the engineering side.

Classifier-free guidance, formally

Section titled “Classifier-free guidance, formally”

Train a single network on both conditional and unconditional prediction by randomly replacing ccc with a null token ∅\varnothing∅ during training. At sampling:

ϵ~θ(xt,c)=ϵθ(xt,∅)+w[ϵθ(xt,c)−ϵθ(xt,∅)].\tilde\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) = \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing) + w\big[\epsilon_\theta(x_t, c) - \epsilon_\theta(x_t, \varnothing)\big].ϵ~θ​(xt​,c)=ϵθ​(xt​,∅)+w[ϵθ​(xt​,c)−ϵθ​(xt​,∅)].

In score terms this corresponds to sampling from

p~(x∣c)  ∝  p(x) p(c∣x)w,\tilde p(x \mid c) \;\propto\; p(x)\, p(c \mid x)^{w},p~​(x∣c)∝p(x)p(c∣x)w,

i.e. the likelihood term raised to a power. For w>1w>1w>1 this is a sharpened distribution — one that no training procedure produced and that is not the posterior. Empirically it trades diversity for fidelity, and the failure mode at large www (blown-out contrast, saturated colours) is exactly what over-sharpening a likelihood should produce.

The honest summary: it is an unprincipled extrapolation that works extremely well, and the field uses it universally while lacking a satisfying account of why.

Tier 4 · Frontier

Open problems, current results, and what is still contested.

Where things stand, as of 2026

Section titled “Where things stand, as of 2026”

Flow matching has largely displaced the DDPM formulation for new work. Not because it generates better images at equal compute — the objectives are close to equivalent — but because the formulation is simpler to reason about, the straight-path property gives a principled route to few-step sampling, and it extends cleanly to non-Gaussian source distributions and to arbitrary domains.

Few-step generation is close to solved, and the methods are ad hoc. Consistency models train a network to map any point on a trajectory directly to its endpoint, giving one- or two-step sampling. Distillation approaches compress a many-step teacher into a few-step student. Both work; both add a training stage and lose some quality. There is no single clean answer yet.

Transformers won the backbone question. DiT-style architectures dominate at scale for images and video, and the reason is scaling predictability rather than per-FLOP quality.

Diffusion is being tried on discrete data, with mixed results. Diffusion language models are an active area — they promise parallel generation and bidirectional context, which autoregressive models cannot offer. As of 2026 they do not match autoregressive quality at scale, and whether the gap is fundamental or an engineering lag is genuinely unsettled.

Open problems

Section titled “Open problems”

  • Guidance lacks a theory. CFG is essential and the distribution it samples from is not one anybody characterised. Attempts to derive principled alternatives have not displaced it.

  • The sampling-quality trade-off is not understood. We know more steps help up to a point. Nothing predicts how many a given model and dataset need.

  • Likelihood and sample quality diverge. Models with better likelihoods often produce worse-looking samples. FID is the standard metric and is known to be biased by its Inception backbone and sensitive to sample count.

  • Memorisation. Diffusion models provably reproduce training examples under some conditions, particularly for duplicated data. The relationship between dataset duplication, model capacity, and memorisation is only partly mapped, and it has direct legal consequences.

  • Discrete diffusion is unresolved. The Gaussian machinery does not transfer cleanly to tokens, and the several proposed corruption processes for discrete data have no clear winner.
