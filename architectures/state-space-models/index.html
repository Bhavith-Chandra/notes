State Space Models

A state space model is a recurrent network with the recurrence chosen so it can also be evaluated in parallel. That single property — recurrent at inference, convolutional at training — is what let SSMs escape the reason RNNs lost. Mamba then added input-dependent state updates, which fixed the quality gap and broke the parallel form, requiring a hardware-aware scan to recover it. The remaining weakness is exact recall, and it is structural rather than incidental.

What you should walk away with

  • The trick is dual evaluation. With a linear time-invariant recurrence, the whole sequence can be computed as a single convolution — parallel like a transformer at training time, O(1)O(1)O(1) per token at inference like an RNN.
  • Selectivity fixed the quality gap and cost the convolution. Making AAA, BBB, CCC input-dependent lets the model decide what to remember; it also makes the system time-varying, so the convolution no longer exists and you need a parallel scan instead.
  • A fixed-size state is the whole trade. Attention keeps every token and can retrieve any of them exactly. An SSM compresses history into dstated_{\text{state}}dstate​ numbers. That is why SSMs win on throughput and lose on copying.
  • SSMs and attention are formally related. Mamba-2’s structured state space duality shows both are instances of one framework with different structure imposed on the same semiseparable matrix — so the choice is about which structure, not about two unrelated ideas.
Tier 1 · Intuition

Plain language. No equations, no prerequisites.

Taking notes versus keeping the transcript

Section titled “Taking notes versus keeping the transcript”

Two ways to follow a long lecture.

Keep the full transcript. Any time you need something, look it up exactly. This is attention. Perfect recall, and the cost of consulting your notes grows with how long the lecture has been.

Keep a running summary. Each new sentence, update your notes — add what matters, let the rest go. This is a state space model. Your notes never get longer, so each update costs the same whether you are ten minutes or ten hours in. But the exact wording of a sentence from an hour ago is gone unless you happened to write it down.

That is the entire trade-off, and everything else on this page follows from it.

Why RNNs lost, and what changed

Section titled “Why RNNs lost, and what changed”

Classic RNNs also kept a running summary, and they were abandoned around 2017 for a specific reason: you cannot parallelise them across the sequence. To compute the state at position 500 you must first compute 499. On a GPU that is fatal — transformers process all positions at once, so they train vastly faster on the same hardware.

The SSM insight is that if you restrict the recurrence to a particular linear form, the same computation can be written as a convolution — and convolutions parallelise perfectly.

So you train it as a convolution (fast, parallel) and run it as a recurrence (fast, constant memory). Same weights, two evaluation modes. That is what put recurrence back on the table.

Why linearity is not a crippling restriction

The recurrence is linear, but the model is not. Nonlinearity sits between SSM layers, exactly as it sits between attention layers in a transformer. The linear recurrence is a component, not the whole model.

The complication Mamba fixed

Section titled “The complication Mamba fixed”

A pure linear time-invariant SSM has a problem for language: it treats every token the same way. The update rule is fixed, so it cannot decide that this word is important and that one is filler. On dense, structured signals like audio that is fine. On text it is a real handicap, and it is why early SSMs were strong on long- range benchmarks and weak on language modelling.

Mamba made the update input-dependent — the model looks at the incoming token and decides how much to remember and how much to forget. That closed most of the quality gap.

The cost: input-dependence breaks time-invariance, and without time-invariance there is no convolution. Mamba’s engineering contribution is a hardware-aware parallel scan that recovers parallel training anyway, by keeping the state in fast on-chip memory and never writing the intermediate states out.

Tier 2 · Mechanics

How it actually works. Pseudocode, no heavy maths.

The two views

Section titled “The two views”

The lineage

Section titled “The lineage”

  1. HiPPO (2020). Asks a precise question: if you must compress a signal’s whole history into a fixed-size state, what is the optimal way? The answer is projection onto a basis of orthogonal polynomials, and it yields a specific structured matrix AAA. This is where the initialisation comes from — it is derived, not tuned.

  2. S4 (2021). Makes HiPPO practical. The naive convolution kernel requires powers of a large matrix; S4 uses a diagonal-plus-low-rank parameterisation to compute it efficiently. Dominated the Long Range Arena benchmark, and remained clearly behind transformers on language.

  3. Mamba (2023). Makes BBB, CCC, and the discretisation step Δ\DeltaΔ functions of the input. Language quality becomes competitive with transformers at comparable scale. Requires the hardware-aware scan to stay trainable.

  4. Mamba-2 (2024). Proves the structured state space duality — that attention and SSMs are two structural specialisations of the same object — and uses it to get a much faster implementation built on matrix multiplication.

Selectivity, concretely

Section titled “Selectivity, concretely”

Consider a copying task: read a sequence, then reproduce a specific earlier token on demand.

A time-invariant SSM applies the same update to every token. It has no mechanism to say “hold this one”. It must compress everything uniformly, and the target token decays along with the filler.

A selective SSM computes Δt\Delta_tΔt​ from the input. Large Δt\Delta_tΔt​ means “this matters, update the state strongly”; small Δt\Delta_tΔt​ means “ignore, keep the state as is”. The model gains an input-conditional gate on its own memory.

Selectivity narrows the gap; it does not close it

Even with perfect gating, the state is dstated_{\text{state}}dstate​ numbers. If a task requires reproducing 5,000 tokens verbatim, and the state holds the equivalent of far fewer, the information is not there. Attention has it, because attention keeps everything. This is an information-capacity argument, not an optimisation one — which is why it does not go away with better training.

Tier 3 · Formal

The objective, the equations, the guarantees.

The continuous system

Section titled “The continuous system”

A linear state space model in continuous time:

h′(t)=A h(t)+B x(t),y(t)=C h(t)+D x(t),h'(t) = A\,h(t) + B\,x(t), \qquad y(t) = C\,h(t) + D\,x(t),h′(t)=Ah(t)+Bx(t),y(t)=Ch(t)+Dx(t),

with h(t)∈RNh(t) \in \mathbb{R}^{N}h(t)∈RN the hidden state. This is classical control theory — the machinery is a century old, and the contribution of this literature is what to do with it.

Discretisation

Section titled “Discretisation”

To use it on a token sequence, discretise with step size Δ\DeltaΔ. The zero-order-hold discretisation gives

Aˉ=exp⁡(ΔA),Bˉ=(ΔA)−1(exp⁡(ΔA)−I) ΔB,\bar A = \exp(\Delta A), \qquad \bar B = (\Delta A)^{-1}\big(\exp(\Delta A) - I\big)\,\Delta B,Aˉ=exp(ΔA),Bˉ=(ΔA)−1(exp(ΔA)−I)ΔB, ht=Aˉ ht−1+Bˉ xt,yt=C ht.h_t = \bar A\, h_{t-1} + \bar B\, x_t, \qquad y_t = C\,h_t .ht​=Aˉht−1​+Bˉxt​,yt​=Cht​.

Δ\DeltaΔ has an interpretation worth carrying: it is the resolution at which the model samples the underlying continuous signal. Large Δ\DeltaΔ means each token is a big jump, so the state turns over quickly and old information decays fast; small Δ\DeltaΔ means fine sampling and long memory. Making Δ\DeltaΔ input-dependent is therefore making memory length input-dependent.

The convolutional form

Section titled “The convolutional form”

Unroll the recurrence from h−1=0h_{-1} = 0h−1​=0:

ht=∑k=0tAˉ k Bˉ xt−k⟹yt=∑k=0tCAˉ kBˉ xt−k.h_t = \sum_{k=0}^{t} \bar A^{\,k}\,\bar B\, x_{t-k} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad y_t = \sum_{k=0}^{t} C\bar A^{\,k}\bar B\, x_{t-k}.ht​=k=0∑t​AˉkBˉxt−k​⟹yt​=k=0∑t​CAˉkBˉxt−k​.

That is a convolution y=Kˉ∗xy = \bar K * xy=Kˉ∗x with kernel

Kˉ=(CBˉ, CAˉBˉ, CAˉ2Bˉ, …, CAˉL−1Bˉ)∈RL.\bar K = \big(C\bar B,\ C\bar A\bar B,\ C\bar A^2\bar B,\ \dots,\ C\bar A^{L-1}\bar B\big) \in \mathbb{R}^{L}.Kˉ=(CBˉ, CAˉBˉ, CAˉ2Bˉ, …, CAˉL−1Bˉ)∈RL.

Once Kˉ\bar KKˉ is known, the convolution costs O(Llog⁡L)O(L\log L)O(LlogL) by FFT and is fully parallel. The equivalence requires Aˉ,Bˉ,C\bar A, \bar B, CAˉ,Bˉ,C to be independent of ttt — the moment they depend on the input, Kˉ\bar KKˉ is different at every position and the convolution does not exist.

S4’s structured parameterisation

Section titled “S4’s structured parameterisation”

Computing Kˉ\bar KKˉ naively needs LLL powers of an N×NN\times NN×N matrix: O(N2L)O(N^2 L)O(N2L) time and O(NL)O(NL)O(NL) memory, which is prohibitive.

S4 restricts AAA to a diagonal plus low-rank (DPLR) form,

A=Λ−PQ∗,A = \Lambda - P Q^{*},A=Λ−PQ∗,

for diagonal Λ\LambdaΛ and low-rank PQ∗PQ^*PQ∗. Under this structure the kernel’s generating function can be evaluated via a Cauchy kernel, reducing the cost to O~(N+L)\tilde O(N + L)O~(N+L). Later work (S4D) showed the purely diagonal case captures most of the benefit, which considerably simplified implementations.

The HiPPO matrix supplies the initialisation. HiPPO derives the AAA that makes the state an optimal projection of the input history onto Legendre polynomials under a chosen measure. Initialising randomly instead measurably destroys long-range performance — this is one of the rare cases where an initialisation is derived from first principles and demonstrably matters.

Mamba’s selection mechanism

Section titled “Mamba’s selection mechanism”

Make the parameters functions of the input:

Bt=sB(xt),Ct=sC(xt),Δt=softplus⁡(sΔ(xt)),B_t = s_B(x_t), \qquad C_t = s_C(x_t), \qquad \Delta_t = \operatorname{softplus}\big(s_\Delta(x_t)\big),Bt​=sB​(xt​),Ct​=sC​(xt​),Δt​=softplus(sΔ​(xt​)),

with sB,sC,sΔs_B, s_C, s_\DeltasB​,sC​,sΔ​ linear projections. The recurrence becomes time-varying:

ht=Aˉt ht−1+Bˉt xt.h_t = \bar A_t\, h_{t-1} + \bar B_t\, x_t .ht​=Aˉt​ht−1​+Bˉt​xt​.

Note AAA itself stays input-independent; the time-variation enters through Δt\Delta_tΔt​ in Aˉt=exp⁡(ΔtA)\bar A_t = \exp(\Delta_t A)Aˉt​=exp(Δt​A). The gating interpretation is direct:

Δt→∞  ⟹  Aˉt→0, state resets, input dominates,\Delta_t \to \infty \implies \bar A_t \to 0,\ \text{state resets, input dominates},Δt​→∞⟹Aˉt​→0, state resets, input dominates, Δt→0  ⟹  Aˉt→I, state persists, input ignored.\Delta_t \to 0 \implies \bar A_t \to I,\ \text{state persists, input ignored}.Δt​→0⟹Aˉt​→I, state persists, input ignored.

The selective scan. Since the convolution is gone, Mamba computes the recurrence with a parallel associative scan — the same primitive as a parallel prefix sum, O(log⁡L)O(\log L)O(logL) depth. The engineering contribution is keeping the expanded state (B×L×D×NB \times L \times D \times NB×L×D×N) in SRAM and recomputing it in the backward pass rather than materialising it in HBM, which is the same IO-aware idea that makes FlashAttention fast.

Structured state space duality

Section titled “Structured state space duality”

Mamba-2’s theoretical result. Write the SSM’s input-output map as a matrix MMM with y=Mxy = Mxy=Mx. From the unrolled recurrence,

Mij=Ci⊤(∏k=j+1iAˉk)Bˉjfor i≥j,Mij=0 otherwise.M_{ij} = C_i^{\top}\left(\prod_{k=j+1}^{i} \bar A_k\right)\bar B_j \quad \text{for } i \geq j,\qquad M_{ij}=0 \text{ otherwise}.Mij​=Ci⊤​​k=j+1∏i​Aˉk​​Bˉj​for i≥j,Mij​=0 otherwise.

This is a semiseparable matrix: every submatrix drawn from the lower triangle has rank at most NNN. Meanwhile masked attention is

Mattn=(L∘QK⊤),M^{\text{attn}} = \big(L \circ QK^{\top}\big),Mattn=(L∘QK⊤),

with LLL the causal mask. Both are structured matrices with a rank constraint on their off-diagonal blocks; the SSM imposes a sequential-product structure and attention imposes a low-rank one.

Practically, this let Mamba-2 restructure the scan into block-matrix multiplications that hit tensor cores, giving a large speedup over Mamba-1 — the theory paid off as an implementation.

The capacity argument, made precise

Section titled “The capacity argument, made precise”

Consider associative recall: store kkk key-value pairs, then answer a query.

  • Attention stores all kkk pairs in the KV cache. Retrieval is exact for any kkk, at the cost of O(k)O(k)O(k) memory.
  • An SSM has NNN state dimensions. It can store at most O(N)O(N)O(N) pairs before interference. For k≫Nk \gg Nk≫N, retrieval degrades — not because of a training failure but because the information was never retained.

This is why SSMs underperform on tasks requiring verbatim copying from long context, and it is a capacity bound rather than a tuning problem. It also predicts the fix: interleave a few attention layers, which is exactly what hybrids do.

Tier 4 · Frontier

Open problems, current results, and what is still contested.

Where this stands, as of 2026

Section titled “Where this stands, as of 2026”

Hybrids are winning, and that is the honest headline. Jamba and similar architectures interleave a small number of attention layers among many Mamba layers — typically around one in eight. The attention layers supply exact recall; the SSM layers supply cheap bulk sequence processing. These consistently beat both pure architectures, which tells you the two mechanisms are complementary rather than competing.

Pure SSMs match transformers on perplexity and lag on recall-heavy tasks. The language-modelling loss gap has essentially closed at matched scale. The gap that remains is task-specific and shows up on long-context retrieval, in-context learning with many examples, and anything requiring verbatim copying.

The inference economics are genuinely compelling. Constant memory per token and no KV cache is a large operational advantage at long context, and it is the reason serving-focused teams keep investing here regardless of benchmark parity.

Nobody claims attention is obsolete any more. The 2023-era framing of Mamba as a replacement has settled into a more accurate picture: attention is one structure on a semiseparable matrix, SSMs are another, and real systems use both.

Open problems

Section titled “Open problems”

  • The recall gap is structural. Fixed state cannot hold unbounded context. Any fix either grows the state (losing the efficiency) or adds attention (which is what hybrids do). No third option has appeared.

  • The optimal hybrid ratio is empirical. One attention layer in eight is a measured default, not a derived one. It presumably depends on task and scale, and nothing predicts it.

  • In-context learning is weaker and the reason is unclear. Induction heads — the attention circuits associated with in-context learning — have no clean SSM analogue. Whether SSMs implement something equivalent differently, or genuinely lack the capability, is open.

  • The ecosystem gap is real and underrated. Quantisation, LoRA, speculative decoding, and most serving infrastructure were built for transformers. Each needs separate work for SSMs, and that adoption cost is a bigger practical barrier than the quality gap.

  • Scaling laws are not mapped. No SSM equivalent of Chinchilla exists, so compute-optimal training for these architectures is guesswork.
